TOEPLITZ SEAM RECONSTRUCTION FROM THE ORIGINAL OPERATORS
Date: 2026-10-25
Verdict: conditional positive embedded circle reconstruction, but the required Toeplitz and time-covariance lemmas are not proved by the original source

Question
========

Can the previously free primitive seam vector be removed by forming

    H_C = Ran(P_C),             S = P_C u_Sigma P_C | H_C,
    Pi_Sigma = P_C - S S*,

and deriving a multiplicity one Hardy shift directly from the original
Calderon projector P_C and seam unitary u_Sigma?

Exact conditional theorem
=========================

Let P be an orthogonal projection on H, let u be unitary, and put
S = P u P on PH. Then

    S* S = P - P u* (1-P) u P.                              (1)

Consequently S is an isometry exactly when

    (1-P) u P = 0, equivalently u(PH) is contained in PH.   (2)

Assume (2) and assume S is Fredholm with ind(S) = -1.  Then Pi=P-SS*
is the orthogonal projector onto ker(S*) and

    rank(Pi) = dim ker(S*) = 1.                              (3)

No purity assumption is needed for this conclusion.  The Wold decomposition
gives a canonical orthogonal sum

    PH = H_shift direct_sum H_unitary,
    H_shift = direct sum over n >= 0 of S^n Ran(Pi).         (4)

The line Ran(Pi) and the unilateral multiplicity one shift H_shift are
canonical functions of the supplied pair (P,u).  A normalized vector in that
line is still defined only up to phase, but every such vector gives the same
projector and the same subspace.

Because u itself is already unitary on the original H, no external unitary
dilation is required.  For any unit psi in Ran(Pi), put

    e_n = u^n psi, n in Z.                                  (5)

The vectors e_n are orthonormal.  For n>=0 they lie in PH, while for n<0
they lie in (1-P)H.  Their bilateral span

    H_circle = closure span{e_n : n in Z}                    (6)

reduces both u and P.  On H_circle, u is the bilateral shift and P is exactly
the Hardy cut onto n>=0.  Changing psi by a phase changes no operator or
subspace.  This is a canonical embedded rank-one circle sector in the
original Hilbert space.

The additional purity condition

    intersection over n >= 0 of S^n(PH) = {0}                (7)

is needed only for the stronger assertion H_shift=PH, namely that the whole
Calderon range is this multiplicity one Hardy space.  It is not needed to
remove the free psi_Sigma direction or to obtain H_circle.

The sign convention is harmless but must be stated.  In the standard Hardy
orientation, wind(u)=+1 gives ind(PuP)=-1.  If the source spectral-flow
convention has the opposite sign, replace u by u*.  This orientation change
does not supply (2) or (3).

Why index one is insufficient
=============================

The Fredholm formula is

    ind(S) = dim ker(S) - dim ker(S*).

It yields rank ker(S*)=1 only after injectivity, which follows here from the
separate isometry hypothesis.  For example

    S0 = unilateral_shift direct_sum 0_C

has ind(S0)=-1, but it is not an isometry and its cokernel has dimension two.

Even isometry plus one dimensional defect does not prove that the whole
Calderon range is pure:

    S1 = unilateral_shift direct_sum identity_C

is an isometry, has ind(S1)=-1 and rank(1-S1 S1*)=1, but its one dimensional
unitary Wold summand survives in every S1^n H.  Its canonical rank-one shift
part still exists, exactly as in (4).

The smallest index counterprobe can be made with one fixed bilateral unitary.
Let H=l2(Z), let u e_n=e_(n+1), and let P_A project onto

    span{e_0,e_2,e_3,e_4,...}.

For S_A=P_A u P_A on P_A H,

    ker(S_A)=span{e_0},
    coker(S_A)=span{e_0,e_2},
    ind(S_A)=1-2=-1.

Yet S_A e_0=0, so S_A is not an isometry.  The incoming defect has dimension
one while the outgoing defect has dimension two.  This uses the same full
unitary u and shows directly that index -1 does not imply leakage zero.  It
is an operator counterprobe only, not a claim that P_A is the physical TFPT
Calderon projector.

There is also a counterprobe inside the exact scalar circle setting.  Take
the standard Hardy projector C and, for 0<a<1/3,

    b_a(z) = (z-a)/(1-a z),        u_a(z) = z^2/b_a(z).

The symbol u_a is smooth, unimodular, strictly orientation preserving, and
has winding one.  Nevertheless

    ||(1-C) u_a e_0||^2 = a^4(1-a^2),

which is 15/4096 at a=1/4.  Thus C M_{u_a} C is not an isometry although all
index and orientation data agree.  This exact control is recorded in
research-source:10843ef87f7062e60e223846, lines 69-128.  It is the smallest
decisive counterprobe to the inference

    positive generator plus Calderon projection implies isometric shift.

The smallest decisive original-source test is therefore the leakage operator

    L_Sigma = P_C u_Sigma* (1-P_C) u_Sigma P_C.             (12)

Equation (1) says S is isometric exactly when L_Sigma=0.  If (12) is nonzero,
the proposed repair stops immediately.  If it vanishes, the next independent
test is strong convergence S*^n -> 0, equivalently (7), if one wants to
identify all of P_C H with the Hardy sector.  Neither test can be replaced by
spectral flow.

Clock dictionary and the missing quarter offset
================================================

Suppose leakage zero and index -1 are proved for the original pair.  To
reconstruct the claimed first order clock from the supplied tangential
operator B_Sigma, the clean sufficient condition is the strong covariance

    exp(it B_Sigma) u_Sigma exp(-it B_Sigma)
      = exp(it) u_Sigma for every real t,                    (8)

together with strong commutation [B_Sigma,P_C]=0.  The infinitesimal form is
[B_Sigma,u_Sigma]=u_Sigma on a common invariant core, but the domain statement
must be supplied rather than inferred formally.

Under (8), Pi_Sigma=P_C-u_Sigma P_C u_Sigma* commutes with the full time
group.  Its rank-one range is therefore a one dimensional reducing spectral
subspace of B_Sigma.  For a unit psi in that range there is a real epsilon
such that

    B_Sigma psi = epsilon psi.                               (9)

Equations (5), (8), and (9) give directly in the original Hilbert space

    B_Sigma e_n = (epsilon+n)e_n, n in Z.                   (10)

On H_circle this is precisely

    K_epsilon = -i partial_theta + epsilon,

under the Fourier map e_n -> exp(in theta).  The nonnegative part inside
P_C H is its Hardy restriction.  The bilateral circle is already embedded in
the original H; no new product geometry, channel, or dilation is introduced.
The original collar formula alone proves neither (8) nor (9).

The offset epsilon is independent of the index.  For every real epsilon the
same Hardy projector C, the same shift S, the same defect projector, and the
same Fredholm index coexist with B_epsilon=-i partial_theta+epsilon.  If the
original source additionally proved on H_circle that

    exp(2 pi i B_Sigma)=i identity,  with 0<epsilon<1,         (11)

then (9) would give exp(2 pi i epsilon)=i and hence epsilon=1/4.  Neither the
Ward covariance (8) nor the quarter monodromy (11) follows from wind(u)=1 or
SF=1.  They require original operator and boundary-domain statements.

Ordinary versus graded determinant
==================================

There is an exact index-only scalar construction.  Regard a Fredholm S as the
two term complex H --S--> H, with H^0=ker(S) and H^1=coker(S).  With the
even-over-odd convention,

    sdet(1-q | H^bullet)
      = det(1-q | H^0) / det(1-q | H^1)
      = (1-q)^ind(S).

For ind(S)=-1 this is (1-q)^(-1).  Therefore

    (5/4) log sdet(1-q | H^bullet) = -(5/4) log(1-q),

which reproduces the scalar seam factor without choosing a vector direction.
This is a mathematically valid relative graded determinant repair.

It is not the trace prescription used in the original electromagnetic
closure.  paper-source:c439790c9d6f51b9bcaa037f, lines 94-122 and 219-224,
uses the ordinary admissible trace

    -(5/4) tr_adm log(1-q |psi_Sigma><psi_Sigma|)

and explicitly invokes the ordinary rank-one determinant identity
det(1-q |psi><psi|)=1-q.  The later infrared formula in
documents/archive--paper-latex--tfpt-42/sections/
041-section-relative-aps-and-superconnection-setup.tex, lines 215-230, is also
an ordinary Fredholm determinant of a positive trace-class transfer operator.
The STr log in the general 1PI expansion at
documents/archive--paper-latex--tfpt-42/sections/
035-subsection-renormalized-physical-and-scheme-readout-laye.tex, lines
116-128, is a field-statistics supertrace.  It does not define a grading on
ker(S) and coker(S), and it is not connected to the seam Fredholm index.

Final status
============

PROVED CONDITIONALLY: If the original pair (P_C,u_Sigma) satisfies leakage
zero and Toeplitz index -1, Pi_Sigma=P_C-SS* is a canonical rank-one
projector.  Its bilateral u_Sigma orbit is a canonical reducing circle sector
inside the original Hilbert space.  No free psi_Sigma direction and no
external unitary dilation are needed.  Purity is required only to identify
the whole Calderon range with that Hardy sector.

NOT DERIVED FROM THE PRESENT ORIGINALS: The source proves the K^1 class and
spectral flow, but not leakage zero or the strong B_Sigma covariance.  It also
does not prove purity for the whole Calderon range or the quarter monodromy
needed to fix epsilon=1/4.  The d1 and h_Sigma^red minimizations do not fill
these typed gaps.

AVAILABLE BUT NEW PRESCRIPTION: A graded determinant on Fredholm cohomology
does recover (1-q)^(-1) from ind(S)=-1 alone.  The current scalar electromagnetic
formula does not use that prescription, so it cannot be claimed as an
originally derived replacement without changing the action definition.
