1. Covariance, domains and the positive projection
---------------------------------------------------

The strong unitary covariance is the correct domain-safe hypothesis. By the
functional calculus it implies

  u Dom(B)=Dom(B),
  B u xi=u(B+1)xi                         for xi in Dom(B),
  u E_B(Delta) u*=E_B(Delta+1).                         (1)

In particular,

  u P_B u*=E_B((1,infinity)) <=P_B.                     (2)

Therefore S=P_BuP_B|_(P_BH)=u|_(P_BH) is an isometry,

  S*S=I_(P_BH),
  SS*=E_B((1,infinity)),
  Pi=P_B-SS*=E_B((0,1]).                                (3)

No formal commutator on an unspecified core is needed. Conversely, a bare
formula [B,u]=u without common-domain invariance would not justify (1).

2. Index sign and the canonical line
------------------------------------

Since S is an isometry, ker(S)=0. Hence

  ind(S)=-dim ker(S*)=-rank(Pi).                         (4)

The explicit hypothesis ind(S)=-1 therefore gives rank(Pi)=1 with the usual
Hardy orientation. Let psi be any unit vector in Ran(Pi). Since Pi is a
rank-one spectral projection of B,

  B psi=epsilon psi,             0<epsilon<=1.           (5)

The gap excludes epsilon=1 as well: covariance would make u^(-1)psi a
nonzero zero eigenvector of B. Thus 0<epsilon<1. The vector is unique only up
to phase, while its line and projector are canonical.

If the original spectral-flow convention uses the opposite orientation, the
corresponding Toeplitz operator is formed with u* and the index sign reverses.
This convention must be fixed before importing the original value +1.

3. Orthonormality, completeness and the self-adjoint domain
-----------------------------------------------------------

Put e_n=u^n psi for n in Z. Equations (1) and (5) give

  B e_n=(epsilon+n)e_n,
  e_n in E_B((n,n+1])H.                                 (6)

The vectors have norm one and distinct spectral intervals are orthogonal.
Moreover,

  E_B((n,n+1])=u^n Pi u^(-n).                            (7)

The half-open intervals (n,n+1] partition R, so countable additivity of the
spectral measure gives the strong identity

  sum_(n in Z) E_B((n,n+1])=I.                           (8)

Each summand has rank one by (7). Hence {e_n} is an orthonormal basis of all
H, not only of a cyclic subspace.

Under the Fourier unitary e_n maps to exp(in theta). Then u maps to
multiplication by exp(i theta), and the spectral theorem maps B, including
its domain, to

  -i partial_theta+epsilon,
  Dom(B)={sum_n c_n exp(in theta):
          sum_n |n+epsilon|^2 |c_n|^2<infinity}=H1(S1).  (9)

Because 0<epsilon<1, P_B is exactly the Hardy projection onto n>=0.

4. Quarter monodromy and localization scope
--------------------------------------------

If the original operator additionally satisfies

  exp(2 pi i B)=i I,                                     (10)

then (6) yields exp(2 pi i epsilon)=i. The interval 0<epsilon<1 fixes
epsilon=1/4 uniquely. The index alone does not fix this offset.

The reconstruction supplies the circle algebra C*(u) and its ordinary arc
subalgebras inside the original H. It does not identify those arcs with a
spacetime net. It also does not by itself second-quantize the one-particle
pair, identify the original physical time, select the observable subalgebra,
or prove the required OS state and reflection statements.

No condition P_C=P_B is needed. The relation J P_C J=1-P_C belongs only to a
separate self-dual/real polarization setup; it is not an axiom of ordinary
complex CAR quantization and plays no role in (1)--(10).
