1. Independent Kahler-zero check
--------------------------------
On a finite original regulator, let h=h* and ell>0. Then R=exp(-ell h)
is positive selfadjoint. Any differentiable real physical variation of
h within this chart has a selfadjoint transfer tangent X=delta R. Indeed
 delta R=-int_0^ell exp[-(ell-s)h] delta h exp[-s h] ds;
taking its adjoint and changing s->ell-s gives the same operator.
The statement also holds for a real ell variation.

For the Grassmannian graph chart, use the actual standard potential

The ambient form on real tangent vectors is, up to the chosen overall
positive normalization,
 omega_R(X,Y)=2 Im Tr[(I+R*R)^-1 X* (I+RR*)^-1 Y].
For R=R* and X=X*,Y=Y*, both inverse factors equal A=(I+R^2)^-1, so
 omega_R(X,Y)=2 Im Tr(A X A Y)=0.                        (1)
Proof: conjugating the trace gives Tr(Y A X A), which by cyclicity is
Tr(A X A Y). Thus the trace is real. No commutation between X,Y,A is
assumed. The displayed zero proof is correct.

Important scope:
* The induced metric is generally nonzero: Tr(A X A X)=
  Tr[(A^(1/2) X A^(1/2))^2]>=0, strictly positive for nonzero finite X.
  Zero symplectic pullback does not mean a constant potential or zero
  Riemannian volume.
* The original map R(H,Hbar) is not holomorphic merely because H uses
  complex coordinates. Computing partial_H partial_Hbar K(R(H,Hbar))
  is not the pullback of the ambient Kahler form without the required
  holomorphic-map hypothesis. These two operations cannot be substituted.
* The given original h has both spectral signs in its unbounded circle
  version. R=exp(-ell h) may then be unbounded, and logdet(I+R*R) need
  not exist. The finite proof extends only with actual domains, traces
  and cyclicity justified. The bounded Cauchy graph remains meaningful
  in the original source; that fact alone does not justify this logdet.
* A genuinely normal-dependent ordered transfer need not be selfadjoint
  even when each instantaneous h is. Equation(1) is not asserted for
  that broader original dynamical-boson source.
