2:   TFPT Carrier — Boundary Polarisation
3:   ------------------------------------
4: 
5:   v10 upstream layer for the Calderón interface.
6: 
7:   In Paper 1's analytic incarnation, the Calderón polarisation
8:   is the decomposition of a boundary spectral space into a
9:   positive and a negative chirality block,
10: 
11:       H = H_+ ⊕ H_-,
12: 
13:   where the two blocks are spanned by the eigenspaces of the
14:   boundary spectral operator above and below threshold. The
15:   Calderón projector is then the linear projection onto `H_+`
16:   along `H_-`. Its key algebraic property is idempotency.
17: 
18:   This module captures the *algebraic skeleton* of that
19:   construction: a `BoundaryPolarization` is a complementary
20:   pair of submodules of a `K`-module `H`. The projection onto
21:   the positive block (an endomorphism of `H`) is idempotent by
22:   the standard Mathlib lemma `IsCompl.projection_isIdempotentElem`.
23: 
24:   Composing the two bridges
25: 
26:       BoundaryPolarization  →  CalderonProjector  →
27:       CalderonCertificate   →  CarrierInvolution
28: 
29:   feeds the carrier-rigidity chain entirely from a polarisation
30:   datum, modulo `Invertible (2 : K)` (i.e.\ `char K ≠ 2`).
31: 
32:   --------------------------------------------------------------
33:   Scope discipline
34:   --------------------------------------------------------------
35: 
36:   This module formalises the *algebraic step* from a polarised
37:   module to a Calderón idempotent. The analytic content of
38:   Paper 1 — boundary elliptic theory producing the spectral
39:   decomposition `H = H_+ ⊕ H_-` from a Calderón operator on
40:   Sobolev spaces — remains upstream. The structure here is the
41:   Lean-side target for that analytic theorem: a future
42:   formalisation of Paper 1 would produce a
43:   `BoundaryPolarization` from the boundary spectral pair.
