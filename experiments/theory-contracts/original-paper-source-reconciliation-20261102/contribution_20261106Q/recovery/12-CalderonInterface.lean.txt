1: /-
2:   TFPT Carrier — Paper 1 Calderon Interface
3:   -----------------------------------------
4: 
5:   This module makes the Paper 1 input to the carrier-rigidity
6:   chain an explicit Lean *certificate*, rather than something
7:   silently consumed.
8: 
9:   --------------------------------------------------------------
10:   What Paper 1 contributes
11:   --------------------------------------------------------------
12: 
13:   TFPT Paper 1 (Boundary Polarization and the Primitive Kernel)
14:   constructs the carrier involution `ε := ι_C |_E` from the
15:   Calderón polarisation of an admissible one-sided boundary
16:   datum. The output of that construction, restricted to the
17:   finite essential carrier `E`, is an element ε of the
18:   endomorphism ring of `E` satisfying `ε² = id`.
19: 
20:   The carrier-rigidity chain consumes exactly this output. It
21:   does *not* attempt to reformalise Paper 1's analytic content:
22:   the Calderón polarisation, boundary elliptic regularity, and
23:   the operator-theoretic construction of the involution all
24:   live upstream of this project.
25: 
26:   --------------------------------------------------------------
27:   Why an explicit interface
28:   --------------------------------------------------------------
29: 
30:   In the v5 chain, `CarrierInvolution` (in
31:   `InvolutionProjectors.lean`) was the entry point of the
32:   algebraic chain. It is a clean structure but it does *not*
33:   document that ε is meant to come from Paper 1's Calderón
34:   polarisation.
35: 
36:   The reviewer's standing critique is that
37:   this hides an *upstream* dependency: the Lean chain works for
38:   *any* involution, but the TFPT claim is that the involution is
39:   produced by a specific boundary construction. Without an
40:   explicit interface, a reader cannot see at the type-theory
41:   level "this is the place where Paper 1 enters".
42: 
43:   `CalderonCertificate` below is exactly that interface: a thin
44:   structural wrapper around `CarrierInvolution` that names the
45:   Paper 1 origin in its docstring. Any future Lean
46:   formalisation of Paper 1's boundary elliptic theory would
47:   *produce* a `CalderonCertificate`; the present chain
48:   *consumes* it.
49: -/
50: 
51: import Mathlib.Algebra.GroupWithZero.Invertible
52: import Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Basic
53: 
54: import TfptCarrier.InvolutionProjectors
55: 
56: namespace TFPT.Carrier.CalderonInterface
57: 
58: /--
59: **`CalderonCertificate`** — Paper 1 interface for the carrier
60: involution.
61: 
62: The certificate carries an element `ε` of a ring `A` (in
63: practice, `Module.End K E` for a finite-dimensional `K`-vector
64: space `E`) together with the involutive relation `ε² = 1`.
65: 
66: The intended reading is: ε is the Calderón-induced carrier
67: involution `ι_C |_E` of TFPT Paper 1, restricted to the finite
68: essential carrier `E`. The Lean structure does not formalise
69: the Calderón construction itself; it names where the construction
70: enters the carrier-rigidity chain.
71: 
72: The forgetful map
73: `CalderonCertificate A → CarrierInvolution A` then feeds the
74: algebraic chain in `InvolutionProjectors.lean`.
75: -/
76: structure CalderonCertificate (A : Type*) [Ring A] where
77:   /-- The Calderón-induced carrier involution element. -/
78:   eps : A
79:   /-- Involutive relation `ε² = 1`, the output of Paper 1's
80:       boundary polarisation argument. -/
81:   eps_sq : eps * eps = 1
82: 
83: namespace CalderonCertificate
84: 
85: variable {A : Type*} [Ring A]
86: 
87: /-- A Calderon certificate produces the algebraic carrier
88: involution consumed by `InvolutionProjectors`. -/
89: def toCarrierInvolution (c : CalderonCertificate A) :
90:     TFPT.Carrier.InvolutionProjectors.CarrierInvolution A where
91:   eps := c.eps
92:   eps_sq := c.eps_sq
93: 
94: end CalderonCertificate
95: 
96: /-- Conversely: a `CarrierInvolution` can be re-packaged as a
97: `CalderonCertificate`, with the understanding that its origin is
98: *not* certified by the wrapper — only that its algebraic content
99: is the same.
100: 
101: This is the converse direction. It is sometimes useful to wrap
102: a `CarrierInvolution` constructed by other means (e.g.\ from a
103: concrete matrix) into the Calderón-interface shape for
104: downstream uniformity. -/
105: def CalderonCertificate.ofCarrierInvolution
106:     {A : Type*} [Ring A]
107:     (c : TFPT.Carrier.InvolutionProjectors.CarrierInvolution A) :
108:     CalderonCertificate A where
109:   eps := c.eps
110:   eps_sq := c.eps_sq
111: 
112: end TFPT.Carrier.CalderonInterface
