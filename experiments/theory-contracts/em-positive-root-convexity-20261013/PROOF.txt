Original fixed function
----------------------

tfpt_1_architecture_e8.tex, lemma emclosure, specify
    c=1/(8 pi), p=1/(6 pi), d=48c^4, s=5/4, M=41,
    q(x)=d exp(-2x), u(x)=q(x)(1-q(x))^(-s), phi(x)=p+u(x),
    C=(4/5)c^6 M>0,
    F(x)=x^3-2c^3 x^2+C log(phi(x)).
These inputs and the form of F are fixed assumptions. make_F's c3v argument
is unused in the source; its default Nfac=8 selects c as stated here.
This proof does not silently vary c3v or infer its provenance.

The script contains a corrected-endpoint finite-difference/findroot check.
The manuscript asserts global derivative signs around its numerical critical
point. The following proof needs neither that critical point nor a sign scan.

1. Domain and sign before the cubic becomes positive
----------------------------------------------------
Since pi>3, c<1/24, d<48/24^4=1/6912<1/1000 and p<1/18.
For x>=0, 0<q<=d<1/1000. Since s=5/4<2,
    (1-q)^(-s)<(1000/999)^2<2,
so 0<phi(x)<1/18+1/500<1. Thus F is smooth for x>=0,
and log(phi)<0 throughout. Set t=2c^3. For 0<x<=t,
    F(x)=x^2(x-t)+C log(phi(x))<0.
No root is omitted near x=0. In particular F(t)<0.

2. Exact log-convexity of the specified seam response
----------------------------------------------------
Put ell=log(u), w=u/(p+u), so 0<w<1. Direct differentiation gives
    ell'=-2-2s q/(1-q),
    ell''=4s q/(1-q)^2>0,
    (log(phi))''=w ell''+w(1-w)(ell')^2>0.
This identity follows by differentiating log(p+exp(ell)); it invokes no
asymptotic estimate of a 'small' term. The checker verifies both identities
symbolically. Equivalently phi is a positive sum of decaying exponentials.

3. Exactly one positive root, and it is simple
---------------------------------------------
For x>=t,
    F''(x)=6x-4c^3+C(log(phi))'' > 8c^3 >0.
Hence F is strictly convex on [t,infinity). As x tends to infinity,
phi tends to p>0, so the logarithm stays finite while the cubic tends to
infinity. Continuity and F(t)<0 imply a root r>t.
At ANY root r>t, the increasing derivative satisfies
    F'(r) > (F(r)-F(t))/(r-t) = -F(t)/(r-t) >0.
After the first root the derivative is positive and increasing, so a second
root is impossible. Equivalently strict convexity and the negative left
endpoint permit only one crossing into positive values.
Therefore exactly one positive root exists and it is simple.

4. Location and scope
---------------------
The existing interval implementation F_iv verifies opposite definite signs at

Together with the analytic proof this brackets the unique positive root.
The checker calls the ORIGINAL F_iv, not a replacement numerical equation.
Its reciprocal is approximately 137.0359992168407. Approximate digits and the
interval enclosure are separate from the global analytic uniqueness proof.
