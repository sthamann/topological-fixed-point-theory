1. Exact free Majorana result

For a single real chiral Majorana fermion, with common conformal circle
scale, the NS and R mode frequencies are n+1/2 and positive integers.
The zeta vacuum energies are -zeta(-1,1/2)/2=-1/48 and
-zeta(-1,1)/2=+1/24. The conformal weight of the spin-structure changer
is the difference, 1/16. For N independent complex chiral fermions
(2N real Majoranas) with a simultaneous half twist, h=N/8. The fixed-A3
family D_n+A3 uses A3≅D3, so N=n+3 and h=(n+3)/8 as in the prior
lattice calculation. The D5+A3 target has 16 real Majoranas and h=1,
D13+A3 has 32 and h=2. Independent multiplicity and simultaneous
twisting are premises here. A partial twist on eight complex channels
inside a larger free theory can also have h=1 without determining the
total channel count.

In charge coordinates bosonize a genuine free complex fermion as
V_{±e_a}, and a full Ramond field as V_s for s=epsilon/2,
epsilon_a∈{±1}. The free vertex OPE is
 V_p(z)V_q(w)=epsilon_cocycle(p,q)(z-w)^(p·q)
             [V_(p+q)(w)+higher terms].
Therefore each elementary fermion has half-integral powers ±1/2 in
its OPE with the spinor. The singular -1/2 branch flips that zero-mode
sign. In the spin-field product, if the sign vectors differ in d
positions, the leading power is (N-2d)/4 and the outgoing weight is
(N-d)/2. In the N=8 target, opposite weights have the identity double
pole, with next coefficient sum_a s_a J_a in unit two-point
normalization. Same-chirality pairs differing in six signs give a
simple-pole D8 root current. For N=16, the corresponding poles are
order four for the identity and order three for the root, compatible
with the spinor having h=2. The other candidate has a local spinor
extension too; its spinor is not a weight-one current.

The even NS D8 algebra contains 120 weight-one currents and does not
already contain the 128 spinor ground states. Adjoining one chosen
Ramond coset yields the 248 E8 weight-one currents. The two possible
D8 spinor cosets s,c both have h=1. They cannot both be adjoined as
mutually local bosonic fields: s=(1/2)^8 and c=s-e8 give s·c=3/2;
each also pairs half-integrally with the NS odd vector v=e8. Summing
partition functions or taking a block-diagonal NS⊕R representation
preserves the sector projectors. All words made of those existing
block-diagonal operations still have zero NS→R block. The physical
single endpoint field, its OPE and its sector-changing domain require
an additional sourced map. A choice of one local extension changes the
operator algebra, not only a sum over energies.

3. Exactly when four marks follow from gluing

For the family locked to the mark count m, use D_(m+1)⊕A_(m-1).
Their discriminant orders are 4 and m. A full diagonal anti-isomorphism
therefore forces m=4. More generally an even unimodular extension
with BOTH factors remaining primitive requires a maximal isotropic
subgroup H with both projections injective. Its order is sqrt(4m),
so |H|≤4 and |H|≤m imply m=4. Rank and type assumptions remain explicit.

Weaker gluing does not force four. A primitive graph of order 2,
generated by (2,m/2), is isotropic precisely when m≡4 (mod 8).
At m=12, (2,6) has qD+qA=1+3=4 and gives a proper index-2 extension
of determinant 12, with both factors primitive. A primitive graph
of order 4, generated by (1,m/4), is isotropic when m≡4 (mod 32).
At m=36, (1,9) has norm 16, order four and resulting determinant 9.
For odd m there is no nontrivial graph injective on both factors
because their orders are coprime.

Unimodularity alone does not force four either. The rank 2m must
be divisible by eight and 4m must be a square, hence m=4k².
For every k≥1 the subgroup generated by (1,k) in Z4×Z_(4k²)
has order 4k and generator norm
 (k²+1/4)+(k-1/4)=k(k+1), even.
It gives an even unimodular overlattice. There is no nonzero isotropic
class in the D factor, so projection of H to the A factor is injective.
The cyclic A image of order 4k is uniquely generated by k.
Norm integrality forces odd D coordinate for its preimage.
Consequently H has pure A subgroup generated by (0,4k), of order k.
The A factor ceases to be primitive for k>1. For example m=16
gives D17+A15, index 8, determinant one, generator (1,2) of h=3.
This is not a counterexample to the two-factor primitive theorem.

The two rank comparisons must stay separate. With A3 fixed,
D13+A3 has mixed even h=1 roots. In the mark-locked family
D_(m+1)+A_(m-1), the prior canonical calculation already finds that
any genuinely mixed norm-two root forces m=4. For vector-type glue,
k(m-k)=m gives (m,k)=(4,2). For spinor-type glue,
4k(m-k)=m(7-m) gives only the mixed cases (4,1),(4,3).
Either sharpened criterion remains conditional on a physically sourced
operator or on independent primitive-factor requirements.
