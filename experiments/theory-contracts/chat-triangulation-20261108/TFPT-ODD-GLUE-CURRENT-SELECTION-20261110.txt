2. Exact discriminator between D5+A3 and D13+A3

For n=5 mod 8, the four diagonal D_n+A3 cosets have minimum squared norms
[0,(n+3)/4,2,(n+3)/4]. The odd glues have n/4 from a D_n half-spinor weight
and 3/4 from an A3 fundamental (or conjugate) weight. The even nonzero glue
has 1+1 from the D_n vector and the A3 wedge-square. This gives
h_min=[0,(n+3)/8,1,(n+3)/8]. The glue is even and integral in both examples;
the integer h shift does not affect the conformal spin modulo 1.

D5: roots by grades [52,64,60,64], full weight-one [60,64,60,64].
D13: roots by grades [324,0,156,0], full weight-one [340,0,156,0].
D13+A3 has D16 weight-one root system and full even self-dual lattice D16+.
Its two odd glue sectors have h_min=2, each with 4096×4=16384 ground vectors.
Its level-one A3 currents are the same 12 root and 3 Cartan fields as in
D5+A3. Its grade-two mixed vector currents (26,6) have h=1 too, as do
(10,6) in D5+A3. Both lattice VOAs are positive/unitary and local in the
usual lattice construction. These properties do not distinguish them.
Requiring an *odd original glue grade*, *both charges*, *h=1*, and an
actual local source realization selects n=5 in the stated family. The
first three conditions are lattice data, the last source origin remains open.
The labels h=1 and h=2 use the conformal generator, not an unproved
identification with the holonomy-shifted time or with 4D physical mass.
The family A3 rank or a 4-dimensional A3 module does not by itself count
three light 4D families.

Use the actual carrier Y=(-1/3,-1/3,-1/3,+1/2,+1/2) on the first five D
directions with zero extension. Use A3 simple coroot A=(1,-1,0,0).
For roots r at weight one define their charges Y(r) and A(r) by pairings.
Across the two full root sets the unnormalized quadratic traces coincide,
sum Y²=50, sum A²=120, sum YA=0. Both root systems have h∨=30 and the
normalized Cartan OPE is the same for the same embedded generators
(Y² norm 5/6, A² norm 2). Uniform-root normalized moments divide by
different root counts 240 and 480; the statement of equality is only for
the unnormalized traces and the independent affine-level comparison.
The fourth traces distinguish them:
  sum Y⁴           = 25 versus 145/9.
  sum Y² A²        = 20 versus 20/3.
  Y²A² by grades   = [0,20/3,20/3,20/3] versus [0,0,20/3,0].
The grade-2 mixed contribution is identical. The extra joint response is
entirely from the two odd grades. This is a finite sourced root census,
not the physical matter beta function or a calculation of alpha.
It supplies a charge-sensitive selection test if the corresponding mixed
marked operation is derived on the original source.

4. Chirality and threshold conditions

Under the canonical block embedding Spin(10)×Spin(16) in Spin(26) the
bare half-spinor is (16,128_s)⊕(bar16,128_c). The complete 4096 states
have 128 of each Spin(10) family chirality. Restricting to the usual SM
with unchanged Y gives net chirality zero. This follows also for any
Spin(10) homomorphism acting through the orthogonal 26-vector: D5
fundamental irreducible dimensions are 10,45,120,16,16. The minimum
dimension at a sum of two fundamental weights is 54, by exact Weyl
dimension and monotonicity. The complex 16 and its conjugate require
32 dimensions in a real representation. Thus the real 26-vector
restriction is at most two copies of the 10 and has a fixed real line.
Clifford multiplication by a vector on that line intertwines the two
Spin(26) half-spinors, which are complex conjugates. Their restrictions
are therefore self-conjugate. This does not exclude direct SM embeddings
without Spin(10), an independent chiral projection/index, or an actual
interacting mirror-decoupling mechanism.

With unbroken SM charges, bilinear invariant masses remove a representation
and its conjugate together. Therefore even if arbitrary heavy quadratic
thresholds are allowed, a full untreated D13 half-spinor cannot give
three net chiral SM families by that route. On the other hand local anomaly
cancellation by itself does not select D5. Both D5 and D13 have no cubic
Lie invariant in the Spin factors. The untreated Spin(26) restriction
cancels SM anomalies by conjugate pairing, and the ordinary Spin(10)
16 is SM-anomaly-free. If the full SU(4) family group is gauged in 4D
with one chiral fundamental product, its cubic anomaly is proportional to
16 (D5) or 4096 (D13), in units of one fundamental, with a sign set by
whether 4 or bar4 is chosen. Both need extra treatment; paired conjugates
cancel. The local chiral A3 level in 2D is a different anomaly notion.

An exact bare charged-spectrum check gives Tr_16 Y²=10/3 while the complete
Spin(26) half-spinor gives Tr_4096 Y²=2560/3, a factor 256 before thresholds.
This would alter a 4D matter contribution if all these fermions were
identified and light, an additional premise. Current counts 248 vs 496
are counts of conformal h=1 algebra states. They are not counts of physical
4D light particles. A regulator/geometry plus actual chiral mass or gap
selection is needed on either side. The rank choice and alpha problem are
not closed by the finite representation checks.
