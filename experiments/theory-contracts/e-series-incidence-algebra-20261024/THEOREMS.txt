Complete finite cut geometry and lossless tetrahedral reconstruction

1. Object, equivalence and requested comparison

Use exactly the standard E8 roots specified in the user's independent
report: 112 roots +/-ei+/-ej and 128 even-parity half roots. Inner products
and operators are evaluated in doubled integer coordinates. The group is
the actual root-reflection Weyl group W(E8). An E6 cut is the root complement
of a regular A2 root subsystem; an E7 cut is the complement of a root line.
A tetrahedron is the four A2 faces of a regular A3 normal subsystem,
equivalently its four E6 complements, with common D5 root core.

The equivalence is the actual Weyl action transporting all marks. A D8
compiler frame belonging to a tetrahedron must contain BOTH its A3 normal
and its common D5 core. Bare A3 containment is a different incidence relation.
No new field, physical source or preferred corner is assumed.

The user supplied the report's statements, without its linked archive.
The present checker reconstructs and verifies those statements independently
from the root definition. It does not certify bytes of an unseen archive.
The earlier joint-source files are retained unchanged and made available in
the accompanying portable bundle, including all their actual dependencies.

2. Complete census and a necessary distinction about frames

The exact orbit construction gives

   E7 cuts                 120
   E6 cuts                1120
   D8 frames               135
   A3 tetrahedra          7560
   tetrahedron/E6 flags  30240
   E6/E7 flags            3360.

There are four E6 cuts per tetrahedron, 27 tetrahedra per E6 cut, three E7
cuts per E6 cut, and 28 E6 cuts per E7 cut. Each D8 compiler frame contains
56 tetrahedra in the sense of the FULL D5+A3 flag. Each full flag determines
exactly one of the 135 D8 frames. Thus 135*56=7560 counts full compiler flags.

There are 616 A3 root subsystems INSIDE a D8, rather than only 56. The 56
coordinate D3's use three coordinates. A second type is an A3 chain using
four coordinates: C(8,4)*8=560. These second-type A3's have D4 complement
inside that D8, while their full E8 complement is D5. Consequently a bare
A3 normal lies in 11 D8 root systems: 135*616/7560=11. Requiring its actual
D5 core as well singles out its one compiler frame. This qualification is
essential when interpreting the map tetrahedra -> D8 frames.

The checker generates all 135 and 7560 actual orbits and checks these
incidences individually; the product identities alone are not its proof.
The original rank32 projectors, 128/96=4/3 and norms5/4+3/4 remain the
intermediate D8-polarized statements proved in COMPILER-CLOSURE.txt.
The rank quotient does not by itself rescale an independently defined scalar.

4. Two incidence transforms on the SAME E6 carrier

Let N have rows the 1120 E6 cuts and columns the 120 E7 cuts, with N(E,F)=1
for E subset F. Let M have the same rows and columns the 7560 tetrahedra,
with M(E,T)=1 for E in T. Both are actual Weyl intertwiners because their
entries are defined by root inclusions. Both are independent of field-lift
phases, which cancel in conjugation of root-support projectors.

Two distinct E6 cuts share an E7 precisely when their A2 normals share one
root line. Their generated normal system is then A3 and their intersection
is D5. The shared root line determines exactly one E7; the A3 hull determines
exactly one tetrahedron. There are 45360 such unordered pairs, partitioned
both into 7560 sets of six tetrahedron edges and 120 sets of C(28,2) edges.
Therefore

    N N^T = A1 + 3 I,            M M^T = A1 + 27 I.            (4.1)

The diagonal entries count 3 and 27 incidences. Every off-diagonal entry is
one exactly for the D5 relation, zero otherwise. The checker tests EVERY
entry of both identities, and the unique tetrahedron of every D5 edge.

On the E7 side, N^T N=28I+B, with B the root-line nonorthogonality graph.
It has strongly regular parameters (120,56,28,24). Thus

    spec(B)  = 56^1, 8^35, (-4)^84,
    spec(A1) = 81^1,33^35,21^84,(-3)^1000,
    rank(N)=120,     ker(N^T)=V300 direct-sum V700.

The kernel splitting is explicit. If Pker is the orthogonal projector onto
ker(N^T), then

    P300 = Pker (A2+6I)/24,
    P700 = Pker (18I-A2)/24.                              (4.2)

These formulas follow from the actual eigenvalues18 and-6 of A2 on the
kernel. All five orbital matrices commute, so the formulas are unambiguous.

5. Lossless reconstruction: the additional simplification

Subtracting (4.1) gives the especially small identity

             M M^T = N N^T + 24 I.                         (5.1)

For every real or complex E6 signal f, with the counting inner products,

    ||M^T f||^2 = ||N^T f||^2 + 24 ||f||^2.               (5.2)

Hence the tetrahedron-sum map M^T is injective with lower singular bound
sqrt(24). Unlike the E7 sum map, it loses none of the E6 information.
This result does not need the character table or irreducibility in Section3.
Its exact Weyl-equivariant inverse on its image is

    f = (N N^T+24I)^(-1) M (M^T f).                       (5.3)

Equivalently the Gram spectrum is108^1,60^35,48^84,24^1000 and rank(M)=1120.
An explicit rational polynomial q with q(A1)=(A1+27I)^(-1) is

    q(x) = 10471/276480 - 199x/165888
           +53x^2/2488320 - x^3/7464960.                (5.4)

The checker verifies this polynomial at every distinct eigenvalue of A1.
Thus (5.3) can be evaluated with a degree-three adjacency polynomial.

Reconstruction preserves a supplied signal; it does not create one. The
root geometry is Weyl-transitive and every diagonal of its orbital algebra
is constant. That algebra therefore cannot contain a singleton vertex

The earlier stronger fixed-mark field-stabilizer obstruction also survives.
