MINIMAL OBSERVABLE REALITY: NECESSITIES, COUNTERWORLDS AND DISCRIMINATORS
2026-10-16. User-directed operational comparison; no physical-origin claim.
VERDICT: THREE INEQUIVALENT OPERATIONAL COUNTERMODELS; NO PHYSICAL SELECTION

1. Change of question and level of the claim

The new request supersedes the proposed automatic continuation from the DAG
theorem to an axiom of protected memory. That theorem and the four preceding
starting classes remain valid within their stated assumptions. They are NOT
premises here. We first ask what observable, repeatable distinctions require,
then deliberately retain inequivalent models instead of choosing new rules
to eliminate their ambiguity. No TFPT, quantum, complex, Hilbert, E8, carrier
rank, spacetime, energy or clock structure is a model-selection criterion.

There is no formal theorem about literally every imaginable world until a
meaning of observation and a domain of models are specified. We distinguish:
 L1: consistency of a fully specified mathematical countermodel;
 L2: a conditional necessity theorem over an explicitly stated model class;
 L3: a testable account of the physical world with independently supported
     premises and predictive content not installed in those premises.
This study supplies L1 countermodels and elementary L2 boundaries. No new
physical data were acquired. No L3 result or literature priority is claimed.

2. Minimal vocabulary versus independent empirical assumptions

Begin with preparations/protocol choices, distinguishable report labels,
finite records h, and available continuations q. No microscopic state is
initially primitive. A support answer lists which subsequent records are
allowed; a weighted answer additionally supplies numerical probabilities.
The latter is more structure and is not silently included in the former.

The following operational commitments are separated:
 O1: reports and their equality/difference can be recognized;
 O2: a declared preparation/context can be used again with the same answer
     rule at the stated level (supports or quantitative statistics);
 O3: certain serial continuations are available with fixed typing;
 O4: for a selected test q, each attainable first report is repeated with
     certainty/necessity on an immediate second q;
 O5: two available preparations make different reports of q certain.

O1 is definitional for a nontrivial observable distinction. O2 expresses the
reproducibility to be studied; extending a finite observation to every future
use is a hypothesis, not a consequence of recognizing a report. O3 is a
protocol-composition assumption, not a derivation of global physical time.
O4 and O5 are deliberately STRONGER test conditions than mere observation.
They provide a common sharp benchmark, not the definition of every possible
observation. Exact repeatability is an idealization; finite error tolerance
would need an additional quantitative formulation.

Independence controls:
 - Two written labels with only one attainable report provide no actual
   distinction.
 - A deterministic device can read b correctly and then reset its input to
   zero. It distinguishes preparations0/1 at first use, but q q on input1
   reports10: observation and perfect first discrimination do not imply O4.
 - A one-report device repeats perfectly without a nontrivial distinction.
 - A random first report followed by perfect copying can be repeatable even
   if the original preparation menu contains no two certainty preparations.
 - A protocol with one available interrogation need not allow q q at all.

Thus observation alone does not establish persistent material memory,
probabilities, a physical causal order or unrestricted composition/control.
Comparison of two recorded results needs accessible comparison information
within that experiment; this does not establish an eternally conserved
microscopic carrier or an observer subsystem.

3. Common conditional consequence and its exact strength

Assume the sharp benchmark O1--O5. Histories ending with report0 and report1
have different residual answers to the same continuation q: only0 versus
only1. Consequently they are different operational predictive classes.
There are at least two reachable continuation-distinguishable classes, and
q preserves this result label under uninterrupted repetition.

This elementary residual-distinction lemma uses the same observational
quotient principle as automata minimization, now applied to actual declared
answers. It is a representational consequence of repeatable discrimination,
not a new explanation of the origin of physical states. The quotient does
not supply a microscopic ontology, a material memory, or a unique carrier.

Crucially, result repeatability does NOT imply that the entire post-test
state is fixed or that a branch T_a satisfies T_a squared=T_a. Counterworld:
read a from (a,c), retain a, increment c modulo3; a separate counter readout
distinguishes c. Every repetition reports a, while successive branch maps
keep changing an independently accessible internal coordinate. Nor does the
lemma supply non-disturbance of other tests, a metric duration, an entropy
arrow, energy conservation or a tensor product.

The shared result goes beyond a list of output labels by identifying
distinct continuation classes. It does not go beyond the substantive content
of the assumed repeatability in a way that explains new fundamental physics.

4. Countermodel R: deterministic relational readout traces

Primitive description: two preparation events r0,r1 and a repeatable read
event q. Allowed finite traces after rb are exactly q:b repeated n times,
for every n>=0. Composition concatenates compatible traces. There is no
primitive random choice or probabilistic mixture. This is a complete
deterministic prediction for every experiment in its declared vocabulary,
not an unweighted envelope with unspecified branching probabilities.

Its two residual classes can be represented by B={0,1}; this is a minimal
realization of the trace rule. A read relates b to continued b and report b.
The selective branches M_a are partial identities on {a}. They satisfy
M_a M_a=M_a and M_0 M_1=M_1 M_0=empty. Identity and these branches exhaust
the continued-system maps generated here. In particular they commute.

Preparations occur at the beginning. Erasing/discarding the continuing
system, re-preparing it mid-protocol, arbitrary functions, flips, external
coins and parallel subsystem operations are NOT part of this minimal model.
The common benchmark did not require them. They can be added as explicit
extensions, but that changes the countermodel. R realizes repeatable
distinctions with no nontrivial probability/convex structure or noncommuting
continued-system action. Delta-probability notation can describe it; this
does not make a full probabilistic mixture space necessary.

Discriminator: a controlled rb followed by the opposite report falsifies R
as specified. Reproducible nontrivial statistical mixtures would require an
extension of its preparation menu. Nominal variability alone cannot exclude
every larger deterministic model with hidden preparation contexts.

5. Countermodel I: quantitative classical information with joint access

Primitive quantitative preparation data are distributions p_ab on four
joint report assignments (a,b), a,b in {-1,+1}. All convex mixtures are
allowed. The full joint readout is available. The minimal operational state
is exactly p because joint outcomes distinguish all its entries. Normalized
states form a simplex with four vertices, affine dimension3, unique pure
mixture coefficients. Real probabilities and randomization are explicit
model assumptions, not derived from observation.

X_a(p)_cd=indicator(c=a) p_cd; Z_b similarly filters d=b. Total X and total Z
are identity channels; selective X/Z branches commute. They repeat their own
results, preserve the other value, and permit joint readout/parity Y=ab.
Allow stochastic transformations and resets as an explicit extension beyond
readout. Erasure and observation then coexist, so observation does not require
global information conservation under every allowed transformation.

Define even preparation=(delta_+++delta_--)/2 and odd preparation=
(delta_+-+delta_-+)/2. Their individual X and Z marginal distributions agree,
but parity is always+1 in the first and-1 in the second. The serial protocol
X,Z,X preserves the first X report with probability1.

Discriminator: a genuine loss of the earlier X record caused by intermediate
Z falsifies these specified nondisturbing I instruments. It does not falsify
all classical dynamics; an ordinary classical disturbing instrument is an
alternative explanation.

6. Countermodel D: operation-generated square with repeatable disturbance

Specify report probabilities and continued responses rather than importing
a particle or quantum algebra. The complete normalized single-system
response profile is (x,z) in [-1,1]^2, with
 P(a|X)=(1+a x)/2, P(b|Z)=(1+b z)/2.
All square-corner preparations, convex mixtures, square symmetries and the
following instruments are admitted, closed under finite serial/adaptive
composition and probabilistic coarse-graining. These choices define a
counterworld; they are not claimed logically necessary.

Use unnormalized v=(t,x,z), t>=0, |x|<=t, |z|<=t. The mass functional is t.
 X_a(v)=((t+a x)/2,(a t+x)/2,0),
 Z_b(v)=((t+b z)/2,0,(b t+z)/2).
Each branch is positive: s=(t+a x)/2>=0 and the output is (s,a s,0), a cone
element. The branch masses sum to t. Subsequent same-test outcomes repeat
with certainty, including X_a squared=X_a and X_-a X_a=0 here. Those stronger
idempotences are choices in this example, not the general lemma of section3.

Total channels are X(v)=(t,x,0) and Z(v)=(t,0,z). Each destroys the other
coordinate. The total channels commute (both orders give (t,0,0)); selected
branches generally do NOT. Do not report all measurement orderings as
noncommuting. For example X_+ Z_+(1,0,0)=(1/4,1/4,0), whereas
Z_+ X_+(1,0,0)=(1/4,0,1/4), distinguishable by a subsequent readout.

After X gives a, the normalized profile is (a,0); after Z gives b it is
(0,b); the last X is uniform. Thus X,Z,X preserves the first X result with
probability1/2, including on every corner preparation. This distinguishes D
from the specified I model through an actual common serial protocol.

D has four extreme profiles but affine dimension2. Its diagonal mixtures
are equal: (++ + --)/2=(+- + -+)/2=(0,0). Every allowed continuation acts
linearly on unnormalized profiles, so those mixtures give identical answers
for ALL allowed protocols, not just a sampled list of words. Their images
in I have different parity. An affine parity effect on D is impossible:
every affine g satisfies g(++)+g(--)=g(+-)+g(-+), contrary to the desired
parity indicator values1,1,0,0. Similarly no sharp joint X/Z readout exists:
its ++ branch would take corner values1,0,0,0, violating the same identity.

The simplex I and square D cannot be isomorphic preserving mixtures and
all operational answers: their affine dimensions and decomposition fibers
differ. R has only two predictive classes and no mixture operation, so is
inequivalent to either enriched theory. Embedding R as a restricted fragment
of I does not make the complete declared theories equivalent. The models
are deliberately different operational predictions, not three incompatible
claims about the hidden ontology of Nature.

D uses the familiar single-system square response geometry of generalized
probabilistic frameworks, not a derived quantum system. Only the generated
operations above are admitted: positivity on this cone does not establish
validity of every positive map on arbitrary multipartite composites. No
bipartite, spacetime, Bell, locality or thermodynamic completion is claimed.

Indeed D has a classical hidden implementation for these generators: keep
two hidden bits; an X read reports the first and independently reseeds the
second uniformly, with the roles exchanged for Z. Readout marginals and
their future answers reproduce D. Different corner-mixture procedures can
then have different inaccessible hidden parity although D identifies their
operational states. No preparation-noncontextuality condition was imposed.
This is why disturbance or a non-simplex operational quotient alone does
not exclude all classical implementations or establish fundamental quantum
structure. I and D remain distinguishable operational theories because I
admits nondisturbing joint/parity access and D does not.

7. Other non-necessity statements, without resolving the ambiguities

Bare support does not fix a frequency law. Pick a hidden bit once per trial,
read it repeatably, and choose its preparation probability p in (0,1).
Every p has exactly the same single-trial possible strings0^n,1^n, but
different report weights. With independently repeated preparation, p=1/3
and p=2/3 yield different frequencies while all finite support patterns
coincide. Trial independence and probability weights are extra assumptions.
Thus support-observability does not select numerical probabilities. This
does not deny that probability can be used to represent deterministic data.

R is a counterexample to necessary noncommuting continued-system controls.
If normalized discard followed by either certainty preparation is ALSO
freely admitted, resets R0,R1 satisfy R0 R1=R0 != R1=R1 R0. Noncommutativity
then follows immediately, but from that additional control assumption and
already classically; it is not a quantum selection principle.

No physical metric time is fixed by these models. Experiment ordering and
word length are protocol data. Changing all durations while keeping the
same report rules leaves every stated benchmark answer unchanged. A model
without duration data proves only non-selection within this operational
question, not that material experiments require no time to be performed.

8. Finite sequential data admit a classical history realization

THEOREM (standard constructive representation, no novelty claim). Given
finite preparation, action and report sets, horizon H, and nonnegative
normalized sequential probability tables p(a_1...a_n | q_1...q_n), n<=H,
suppose summing over the latest report recovers the prefix table and earlier
marginals do not depend on later choices. Then a finite classical stochastic
history machine realizes every table, including adaptive protocols.

Proof: a machine state is the preparation label and full history
h=(q_1,a_1,...,q_k,a_k). When q is chosen, output a with conditional weight
p(h,q,a)/p(h), then append (q,a) to h. For unreachable zero-probability h,
choose any normalized conditional. At horizon H halt. Induction telescopes
the product of conditionals to the given table. There are finitely many
histories, bounded by |preparations| sum_(k=0)^H (|actions||reports|)^k.
The model may depend on the entire history and preparation context. For
support data use permitted branches in place of positive probabilities,
assuming analogous prefix extension consistency.

This gives more than the initial definitions: arbitrary finite tables in
this class have an explicit classical representation. It explains an
identification LIMIT, not the origin of the physical carrier. It is not a
claim of an efficient simulator, bounded stationary memory, reversible
dynamics, preparation noncontextuality or spacelike locality. The prefix
condition supplies a direction to the experiment; it does not derive
physical causality or forbid feedback with memory. Constraints on resources,
independent preparations, causal access or operational equivalences can
make classical competitors testable; do not drop those constraints when
interpreting such a test. Nothing here overturns Bell or contextuality tests.

Finite sequential data can distinguish I from D, yet cannot on these
assumptions exclude ALL classical hidden-memory implementations of D.
Those are different identification questions. Finite precision also cannot
prove exact all-context equality of two empirical preparation procedures.

9. What can select a remaining model, and what is actually necessary?

Discriminators are predictions, not new measurements performed this round:
 - R versus quantitative extensions: reproducible nontrivial weights under
   a specified preparation, conditional on its operational controls.
 - Specified I versus D: X,Z,X match rate1 versus1/2; disturbance can be
   classical too, so this selects between instruments, not ontologies.
 - I versus D correlation access: distinguish the two diagonal mixtures by
   an accessible parity/joint test, or certify their operational equivalence
   relative to a justified family. Mere absence of a found test is no proof
   that no such test is physically possible.
 - Unrestricted classical history models versus stricter alternatives:
   finite sequential disturbance alone does not suffice. A justified memory,
   causal-access or preparation-equivalence restriction is additional
   empirical/operational information. No restriction is adopted just to
   force the desired winner.

All three models share the declared repeatable distinction, nonempty
composable transcripts, and at least two residual answer classes. Those
shared properties are largely the benchmark and its representational
consequences, not a nontrivial new physical necessity. Neither probability
weights, global information conservation, noncommuting controls, simplex
structure, compatible joint readout nor a quantum/Hilbert structure is
forced by that benchmark. The history-realization theorem adds a broad
conditional underidentification statement, not a chosen fundamental model.

No selection of a physical world has been achieved. The next useful input
is a concrete reproducible phenomenon WITH justified operational/resource
constraints that separates candidates. Do not continue by designing a
protected memory or choosing a new axiom merely to remove the multiplicity.
Keep the multiplicity and its discriminators as the current research result.
