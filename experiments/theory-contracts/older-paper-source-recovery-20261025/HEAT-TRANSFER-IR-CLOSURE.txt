3. Exact ground-normalized heat convergence
--------------------------------------------
For heat parameter t>0 set

  T_t=exp(-t|B_a|),
  R_t=exp(t/4) T_t,
  r=exp(-t/2).

In the absolute-energy basis, T_t has eigenvalues exp[-t(2m+1)/4],
and R_t has eigenvalues

  1,r,r^2,r^3,... .                                    (2)

All these are determined by the old given spectrum. Consequently

  ||R_t-P_gap||_1=sum_(m>=1)r^m=r/(1-r).               (3)

Thus R_t tends to P_gap in TRACE NORM as t tends to infinity. On this
original representative the ground-mode limit and its simplicity are
proved, rather than inserted as a free rank-one vector.

The normalization exp(t/4) matters: T_t itself tends to zero in trace norm.
It does not tend to P_gap. Subtracting the known ground energy is a
mathematical ground-energy normalization. Its use in a physical EM source
must be part of the original source prescription, not hidden in notation.

4. Fredholm determinant and controlled rank-one limit
------------------------------------------------------
Let Q be a separate determinant argument with |Q|<1. By (2),

  det(I-Q R_t)=product_(m>=0)(1-Q r^m).                 (4)

The product converges since R_t is trace class. Equation (3), or the
direct convergent product, gives

  det(I-Q R_t) -> 1-Q.                                (5)

For 0<=Q<1 the log error is nonnegative after reversing its sign and obeys

  |log det(I-Q R_t)-log(1-Q)|
    =sum_(m>=1)-log(1-Q r^m)
    <=sum_(m>=1) Q r^m/(1-Q r)
    =Q r/[(1-r)(1-Q r)].                             (6)

Here -log(1-x)<=x/(1-x) and Qr^m<=Qr justify every inequality. This is
an infinite-dimensional bound, not an extrapolation from a finite cutoff.

For a supplied susceptibility exponent b=5/4, raising (4) to the power
-b gives the full transfer factor, whose limit is (1-Q)^(-5/4). Neither
b nor the determinant prescription is derived from the heat spectrum;
they are retained original electromagnetic inputs.

5. The unnormalized full heat determinant
------------------------------------------

Every factor beyond 1-q is nontrivial, so D(q)!=1-q at finite t.
This is not a quantum Fock partition function being postulated; it is
the ordinary Fredholm determinant of the old heat-transfer formula.

The quarter twist is crucial. An untwisted positive Hardy spectrum without

different domains, not two expressions for the same transfer.

------------------------------------------------

derivative of the absolutely convergent product gives

  log F(q)=b sum_(n>=1) sigma_odd(n) q^n/n,
  sigma_odd(n)=sum_{d divides n, d odd} d.

Consequently every coefficient is fixed by
