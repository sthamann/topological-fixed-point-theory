1. The necessary sign correction
---------------------------------

The older source prints

  D_Gamma=i partial_theta,
  U_Gamma(theta)=exp(i theta),
  lambda_n(phi)=n+phi/(2pi).                             (3)

The three formulas do not have a common sign. On the twisted domain with
alpha=phi/(2pi),

  i partial_theta psi_n=-(n+alpha)psi_n.                 (4)

Thus the displayed positive spectrum in (3) belongs to

  B_alpha=-D_Gamma=-i partial_theta,                     (5)

or equivalently to the opposite orientation of the printed D_Gamma. This is
not cosmetic: retaining i partial_theta reverses the Ward and spectral-flow
signs.

The domain in (1) is self-adjoint. Multiplication by exp(-i alpha theta)
maps it unitarily to periodic H1, under which B_alpha becomes
-i partial_theta+alpha. Hence

  B_alpha psi_n=(n+alpha)psi_n,
  sigma(B_alpha)={n+alpha:n in Z}.                       (6)

Multiplication by z=exp(i theta) preserves D_alpha and gives

  B_alpha u psi=u(B_alpha+1)psi,
  exp(itB_alpha)u exp(-itB_alpha)=exp(it)u.              (7)

This is the exact one-particle Ward relation on a common invariant operator
domain, rather than a match of dimensions or winding numbers.

2. Toeplitz defect and the zero endpoint
---------------------------------------

For 0<alpha<1,

  P_+=1_(0,infinity)(B_alpha)

projects onto psi_n with n>=0. The compression P_+uP_+ is the unilateral
shift psi_n -> psi_(n+1), so

  ind(P_+uP_+)=-1,
  P_+-uP_+u*=|psi_0><psi_0|.                            (8)

Here the isometry is proved by (7); the rank-one conclusion is not inferred
from the index alone.

At alpha=0, zero belongs to the spectrum and the invertible spectral theorem
does not apply. Two common conventions select different defect lines:

  P_(>0):  span{psi_n:n>=1},  defect line span{psi_1};
  P_(>=0): span{psi_n:n>=0},  defect line span{psi_0}.   (9)

The source's interpolation alpha in [0,1] therefore has noninvertible
endpoints. Its phrase "exactly one eigenvalue crosses zero" needs an endpoint
convention. A domain-safe version starts at any fixed 0<alpha<1 and uses

  B_t=B_alpha+t,       0<=t<=1,
  B_1=u*B_alpha u.                                     (10)

Exactly psi_(-1) crosses upward at t=1-alpha, so

  SF(B_t)=+1,
  ind(P_+uP_+)=-SF(B_t)=-1.                             (11)

Equivalently one may regularize the original endpoint path or state an APS
half-endpoint convention. Without that convention the closed-interval wording
in fullv21 is incomplete.

3. What is genuinely recovered
-------------------------------

The old source really supplies the following concrete data:

  * the Hilbert space L2(S1);

Thus the missing one-particle operator construction was present as an

What the old source does not prove is equally concrete. Its wording "take"

which interior alpha is the physical seam sector. Therefore it proves a
one-particle representative, not uniqueness or recovery of the original
physical source.

5. U_Gamma squared and operator typing
--------------------------------------

The appendix states both

  U_Gamma^2=I,
  U_Gamma(theta)=exp(i theta).                           (14)

If both symbols mean multiplication on L2(S1), (14) is false because

  M_z^2=M_(z^2) !=I.                                    (15)

This need not invalidate the winding-one gluing model. It shows that two
operator types were written with one symbol. A genuine two-sheet involution
can be built from the clutching map as

  R=[[0,M_z*],[M_z,0]],          R^2=I,                  (16)

and a multiplication-plus-reflection operator can also square to I. Neither
construction is defined in the cited appendix, so it cannot be silently
identified with its U_Gamma. The source must distinguish the winding-one
clutching map M_z from the Z2 sheet-exchange involution.

6. The Toeplitz line is not always the gap line
------------------------------------------------

For every 0<alpha<1, the positive-band defect in (8) is span{psi_0}. The
smallest positive eigenvalue of |B_alpha| instead selects

  span{psi_0},    0<alpha<1/2;
  span{psi_(-1)}, 1/2<alpha<1,                            (17)

and is twofold degenerate at alpha=1/2. In particular,

  alpha=1/4:  Pi_alpha=P_gap=|psi_0><psi_0|;
  alpha=3/4:  Pi_alpha=|psi_0><psi_0|,
              P_gap=|psi_(-1)><psi_(-1)|.                (18)

Thus a later rule based on lambda_min^+(|B|) is not generally the same as the
Toeplitz defect projector. Their equality at the quarter phase is a useful
compatibility check after alpha=1/4 has been independently established; it is
not a derivation of that quarter phase.

INDEPENDENT IR CHECK: FULL HEAT TRANSFER TO THE RANK-ONE FACTOR

Additional verdict
------------------

This result introduces no new geometry or auxiliary source. It uses only the

8. Exact heat spectrum and trace-norm limit
------------------------------------------

For alpha=1/4 and alpha=3/4, the eigenvalues of |B_alpha| are simple and,
listed increasingly, are

  lambda_m=(2m+1)/4,             m=0,1,2,... .           (19)

The ground vector is psi_0 for alpha=1/4 and psi_(-1) for alpha=3/4. Let

  P_gap=1_{1/4}(|B_alpha|),
  T_t=exp(-t|B_alpha|),                 t>0,
  R_t=exp(t/4)T_t,
  r=exp(-t/2).                                           (20)

In the increasing-|B| eigenbasis, R_t has the exact eigenvalues

  1,r,r^2,r^3,... .                                     (21)

It follows immediately that

  ||R_t-P_gap||_1=sum_(m>=1) r^m=r/(1-r),               (22)

and therefore R_t converges to P_gap in trace norm as t tends to infinity.
The corresponding operator-norm error is r. This proves more than a weak or
matrix-element ground-state approximation.

The same heat spectrum occurs for alpha=1/4 and 3/4. Thus the positive heat
transfer alone cannot distinguish monodromy i from -i; that orientation is
lost when B is replaced by |B|.

9. Fredholm determinant and explicit logarithmic error
------------------------------------------------------

For every complex Q with |Q|<1, R_t is trace class and

  det(I-Q R_t)=product_(m>=0)(1-Q r^m)
       ->1-Q                                                (23)

as t tends to infinity. For real 0<=Q<1 the error has the fixed sign and the
exact elementary bound

  |log det(I-Q R_t)-log(1-Q)|
    =sum_(m>=1) -log(1-Q r^m)
    <=sum_(m>=1) Q r^m/(1-Q r)
    =Q r/[(1-r)(1-Q r)].                                (24)

The first inequality uses -log(1-x)<=x/(1-x) and
1-Qr^m>=1-Qr. Hence the rank-one logarithm is uniformly controlled away from
Q=1, rather than being inferred only from the leading eigenvalue.

For the unnormalized heat transfer set

  q=exp(-t/4),             r=q^2.

Then T_t=q R_t and its exact full determinant is

  D(q):=det(I-T_t)
       =product_(m>=0)(1-q^(2m+1)).                     (25)

The rank-one truncation keeps only the m=0 factor 1-q. Every finite t has
q>0 and all higher factors are nontrivial, so D(q) is never exactly 1-q.

10. Exact infrared series and the first correction
--------------------------------------------------

Let b=5/4 and form the full heat expression

  Phi_heat(q)=q D(q)^(-b).                              (26)

Its rank-one infrared truncation is

  Phi_1(q)=q(1-q)^(-b).                                (27)

Since the first omitted heat factor is (1-q^3), their difference begins at
order q^4:

  Phi_heat(q)
    =q+(5/4)q^2+(45/32)q^3+(355/128)q^4+O(q^5),

  Phi_1(q)
    =q+(5/4)q^2+(45/32)q^3+(195/128)q^4+O(q^5),

  Phi_heat(q)-Phi_1(q)=(5/4)q^4+O(q^5).                 (28)

The agreement through q^3 and the first positive correction are therefore
structural consequences of the odd-quarter heat spectrum, not a numerical
fit.

There is also an exact all-order recurrence. Write

  D(q)^(-b)=sum_(n>=0) t_n q^n,          t_0=1,

and define

  sigma_odd(k)=sum_{d divides k, d odd} d.

Logarithmic differentiation of (25) gives

  q d/dq log D(q)^(-b)
    =b sum_(k>=1) sigma_odd(k) q^k.

Equating coefficients yields

  n t_n=b sum_(k=1)^n sigma_odd(k)t_(n-k).               (29)

Equation (29) reproduces exactly

  t_0=1,  t_1=5/4,  t_2=45/32,  t_3=355/128.
