3. Elementary tail bound and theorem

For every E,t>0, the spectral theorem gives
  ||1_(E,infinity)(H_N)v_N||^2
    <= [n_N-F_N(t)]/[1-exp(-tE)].                      (1)

Given eta>0, continuity at zero lets us choose t>0 with n-F(t)<eta/4.
For all large N the numerator is then <eta/2. Choose E large enough so
1-exp(-tE)>1/2, avoiding the discrete limiting spectrum. Equation(1) gives
a tail <eta for all sufficiently large N. Hence
  lim_(E to infinity) limsup_N
       ||1_(E,infinity)(H_N)v_N||^2 = 0.              (2)

For the real-time variant, average Re G_N over 0<t<a. Spectral calculus
gives n_N-a^-1 integral_0^a Re G_N(t)dt
 =integral[1-sin(a lambda)/(a lambda)]dmu_N(lambda).
For lambda>E>=2/a, the bracket is >=1/2. Therefore
  ||1_(E,infinity)(H_N)v_N||^2
    <=2[n_N-a^-1 integral_0^a Re G_N(t)dt].            (2a)
Uniform boundedness |G_N(t)|<=n_N permits dominated convergence. Continuity
of G at0 makes the limiting right side arbitrarily small by taking a small,
and then E>=2/a. This proves(2) from REAL time as well, with no derivative
or first-moment hypothesis.
