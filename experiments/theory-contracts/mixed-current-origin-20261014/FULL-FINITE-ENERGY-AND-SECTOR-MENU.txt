2. A uniform gap for every remaining one-body mode

The actual 16x16 Bloch matrix obeys
  h(p)=H0-sin(p)X+rho Z, rho=1-cos(p),
  H0^3=H0, rank H0=14, ||H0||=1,
  X^2=Z^2=I, {X,H0}={X,Z}=0.
Consequently
  h(p)^2=sin(p)^2 I+(H0+rho Z)^2.
The two zero vectors at p0 are the original top and bottom row vectors;
the restriction of h'(0) is diag(-1,+1).

For 0<=rho<=1/2, fourteen singular values of H0+rho Z are at least
1-rho>=1/2, by the singular-value perturbation inequality. Hence at most
two eigenvalues of h have absolute value below1/2. For 1/2<=rho<=3/2,
sin(p)^2=rho(2-rho)>=3/4, so none has absolute value below1/2.
For 3/2<=rho<=2, ||(H0+rho Z)v||>=(rho-1)||v||>=||v||/2,
so again none does. These cover the entire Brillouin circle.

Thus the third and every later absolute one-body eigenvalue are uniformly
>=1/2 at every momentum. These modes cost at least N/(4pi) in H_N.
This is an all-p inequality, not a numerical grid conclusion.
