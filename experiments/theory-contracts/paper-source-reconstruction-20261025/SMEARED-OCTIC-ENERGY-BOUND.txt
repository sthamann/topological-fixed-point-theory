=========================================================

Verdict
-------

PROVED on the algebraic finite-energy core, in the following exact sense.

The shifted neutral octic has a closable smooth smearing F8(rho), and both
F8(rho) and its adjoint are absolute strong mode sums on every fixed energy
cutoff. There is an energy-independent sequence of globally defined finite
CAR-word operators G_K which eventually agrees with the current-mode cutoff
on every fixed energy sector. The existing QWZ elementary-field theorem
therefore gives the transported iterated limit

  first N -> infinity, then current-mode cutoff K -> infinity.

This already repairs the strong smeared-operator claim. The finite microscopic
operators G_(N,K) are global Fourier operators, so this route does not assert
exact microscopic interval support.

The separately mollified local route also works as an iterated limit. One must
first combine the old point-splitting subtractions into their finite Wick and
divided-difference form. Then take

  N -> infinity at fixed mollifier scale sigma and split epsilon,
  sigma -> 0 at fixed epsilon,
  epsilon -> 0.

This gives the same F8(rho) strongly on the finite-energy core, together with
its adjoint. It does not give a bound uniform in N,sigma,epsilon, a simultaneous
ultraviolet limit, or a point field as a bounded operator.

1. Current algebra and energy convention
-----------------------------------------

Let j^a_n, a=0,...,7, be the eight folded eigenchannel current modes in the
negative-sea alpha=1/4 presentation. On the algebraic finite-energy domain,

  [j^a_n,j^b_m]=n delta_ab delta_(n,-m),
  (j^a_n)^*=j^a_-n.

The eigenchannel quasifree covariance is diagonal, so these channels admit
the usual derived graded tensor-product factorization. They are nevertheless
the eight images of the one fixed folded CAR source, not eight independently
chosen source copies. In the original arc chart their covariance is generally
correlated.

On one fixed charge sector, including the source p=0 sector, the zero modes
are fixed scalars. The shift delta_alpha therefore changes only those scalar
zero-mode entries. Let

  H_osc=sum_(a=0)^7 sum_(n>0) j^a_-n j^a_n

and let P_E be its spectral projection for energy at most E. Additive fixed
ground and charge energies do not change any estimate below.

For a projection in the whole CAR Hilbert space one must also bound the
charge sectors. P_E for H_osc alone would have infinite rank. The actual
positive source generator, in folded energy units, is

  H_source/8 = H_osc + sum_k [q_k^2/2 + delta_k q_k].

Here q_k is an integer and |delta_k|<=15/32 in the alpha=1/4 chart. Therefore

  q_k^2/2 + delta_k q_k >= q_k^2/32.

An actual source-energy cutoff consequently includes only finitely many
charge sectors and bounds |q_k+delta_k| by C sqrt(1+E). The zero modes then
obey the same square-root energy estimate as the nonzero modes. Equations
(9)--(18) extend with one uniform coefficient constant to this full positive
source-energy cutoff. Whenever finite dimensionality or V_N is used below,
P_E means this actual source cutoff, or one explicitly fixed charge sector,
and not H_osc alone with unrestricted charges. No oscillator index is being
identified with a spacetime dimension.

The four positive currents h^c_n=sum_a A_ca j^a_n use the already fixed matrix
A=U_+^T O^T M. Since A is finite and AA*=I4, bounds for j transfer to h with
one fixed constant. The isotropy AA^T=0 is needed for the octic field identity,
but the estimates below do not rely on cancellations from isotropy.

The normalized shifted octic is

  F8^d(z)=:I8(h(z)+b/z):/[96 sqrt(140)],
  b=A delta_alpha.

It is a finite linear combination of normal-ordered current monomials of
current degree r<=8. Every occurrence of b is a scalar zero-mode insertion and
therefore lowers r.

2. One-current energy bounds
----------------------------

For n>0 and psi=P_E psi,

  ||j^a_n psi||^2
    =<psi,j^a_-n j^a_n psi>
    <=E ||psi||^2.                                        (1)

The commutator gives

  ||j^a_-n psi||^2
    =||j^a_n psi||^2+n||psi||^2
    <=(E+n)||psi||^2.                                     (2)

Equations (1) and (2) hold componentwise and hence, with a fixed coefficient
constant, for h^c_n and its conjugate current. They also hold after an earlier
finite string of current modes, with E replaced by the largest intermediate
energy.

3. Polynomial bound for every octic mode
----------------------------------------

Consider a degree-r normal-ordered current monomial of total mode m,

  W_m=sum_(n_1+...+n_r=m) :h^(c_1)_n1 ... h^(c_r)_nr: .    (3)

Zero modes are fixed scalars and can be omitted from the count. For one term
in (3), let

  P=sum_(n_i>0) n_i,
  Q=sum_(n_i<0) |n_i|.

Normal ordering places the positive modes on the right. Acting on P_E, the
term vanishes unless

  P<=E.                                                    (4)

Since m=P-Q,

  Q=P-m<=E+|m|.                                           (5)

Thus only finitely many integer r-tuples can act on P_E. Their number is at
most

  C_r (1+E+|m|)^(r-1).                                    (6)

After all positive modes have acted, the negative modes can raise the energy
by at most Q. Every intermediate energy is bounded by 2E+|m|. Repeated use of
(1) and (2) therefore bounds the norm of each surviving term by

  C_r (1+E+|m|)^(r/2) ||psi||.                            (7)

Combining (6) and (7),

  ||W_m P_E||
    <=C_r (1+E+|m|)^(3r/2-1).                             (8)

For r<=8 the largest exponent is eleven. Since I8 has finitely many fixed
coefficients and b is fixed,

  ||F8_m P_E|| <= C (1+E+|m|)^11,                         (9)

where C depends only on A,b and the chosen normalization. Applying the same
argument to the conjugated polynomial gives

  ||(F8^#)_m P_E|| <= C# (1+E+|m|)^11.                    (10)

Here F8^# is the field adjoint, with conjugated A,b and reversed radial
ordering. Equation (10) is not inferred from (9) by a formal symbol; it is the
same current-mode proof for the actual adjoint field.

4. Smooth smearing, strong convergence and closability
------------------------------------------------------

Let rho be smooth on the fold circle and write rhohat_m for its Fourier
coefficients in the convention paired with F8_m. For every q,

  sum_m |rhohat_m| (1+|m|)^q < infinity.                  (11)

Define on the algebraic finite-energy core D_fin

  F8(rho) psi=sum_m rhohat_m F8_m psi.                     (12)

Equations (9) and (11) make (12) absolutely convergent in Hilbert norm for
every psi in D_fin. The symmetric cutoff

  F8^[K](rho)=sum_(|m|<=K) rhohat_m F8_m                  (13)

satisfies the operator estimate

  ||(F8(rho)-F8^[K](rho))P_E||
   <=C(1+E)^11 sum_(|m|>K)|rhohat_m|(1+|m|)^11 ->0.        (14)

In particular, with

  C_rho=C sum_m |rhohat_m|(1+|m|)^11,

the full smeared field obeys the explicit energy bound

  ||F8(rho)P_E|| <= C_rho (1+E)^11.                       (14a)

The adjoint field obeys the same estimate. Let rho^# denote the smooth test
function obtained from rho by the retained circle/radial adjoint convention;
it includes complex conjugation and the fixed conformal coordinate factor.
On D_fin the mode adjoint identity therefore passes through the absolutely
convergent sums:

  <F8(rho)psi,phi>=<psi,F8^#(rho^#)phi>.                  (15)

Consequently F8(rho) is closable and its Hilbert-space adjoint extends the
displayed smeared adjoint on D_fin. This is the required field-plus-adjoint
statement. It is a statement about smooth smeared fields, not point values.

5. Finite current cutoffs are finite original CAR words
-------------------------------------------------------

In the filled-sea representation each eigenchannel current mode has the
standard fermionic form

  j^a_n=sum_r :chi^a_r* chi^a_(r+n):_sea.                 (16)

For fixed n and fixed input cutoff P_E, only finitely many summands in (16)
act. Far from the Fermi surface both occupations agree with the sea and the
normal-ordered bilinear annihilates the vector. Every excitation in P_E uses
one of finitely many labels, and a shift by n enlarges that label window only
by |n|. Thus j^a_n P_E is a finite sum of original CAR bilinears.

Equations (4) and (5) show more. For fixed m and E, only finitely many current
mode tuples in (3) act, and every surviving current reduces on P_E to finitely
many CAR bilinears. This observation alone does not yet define an acceptable
microscopic approximant: P_E is a comparison projection, not part of the
physical operator.

Define instead a global finite continuum operator G_K(rho), K>=2, without
using E or P_E. Keep only

  |m|<=K for the output field mode,
  |n_i|<=K^2 for every internal current index,
  |r|<=K^4 in every CAR sum (16),                         (16a)

and use the same sea normal ordering, coefficients, b insertions and rhohat_m.
This is a finite sum of globally defined original CAR words of fermion degree
at most sixteen. Its one-particle entries are finite Fourier sums and hence
smooth. For each fixed E, equations (4)--(5) bound every internal current
index that can act by E+K. The source-energy bound also confines all fermion
excitation labels. Consequently there is K_E such that, for K>=K_E,

  G_K(rho) P_E = F8^[K](rho) P_E.                         (16b)

Thus G_K is independent of E, while P_E is used only to state eventual
agreement and convergence.

Replace each elementary continuum CAR Fourier mode in G_K by the corresponding
actual top-row QWZ field Psi_N(g), retaining the same filled-sea ordering and
adjoint. Denote this globally defined finite microscopic Fock operator by
G_(N,K)(rho). The existing microscopic theorem gives for each fixed E,K

  ||(G_(N,K)(rho)V_N-V_N G_K(rho))P_E|| ->0,              (17)

and the analogous statement for the adjoint. The original theorem is stated
vectorwise. Since P_E is finite dimensional, convergence on a basis upgrades
it to the restricted operator norm in (17).

Combining (14) and (17), using that V_N is a contraction, gives the transported
iterated limit

  lim_(K->infinity) limsup_(N->infinity)
   ||(G_(N,K)(rho)V_N-V_N F8(rho))P_E||=0,                 (18)

and the identical conclusion for the adjoint. Equation (18) is the repaired
strong microscopic smeared-composite theorem.

No K-uniform microscopic estimate was used. Therefore (18) does not assert a
simultaneous N,K limit. The finite K approximants are Fourier-mode operators
with global circle support. Equation (18) alone does not prove exact interval
support for the microscopic approximants.

6. Separately mollified local route
-----------------------------------

The original current point-splitting prescription can also be retained
locally, but every elementary fermion must first be separately smeared.

Choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported mollifier eta with integral
one. Then ||eta||_1=1 and therefore |etahat(sigma r)|<=1. Let

  eta_(sigma,x)(y)=sigma^(-1) eta((y-x)/sigma),
  psi_sigma(x)=a(eta_(sigma,x)).                           (19)

Use translated copies of (19) at every point in the epsilon-split definition
of the currents and the octic. Let

  F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)

be the result after the same centre smearing rho and the same old sea
subtractions and derivative counterterms. At finite N the subtraction may be
written with the actual microscopic filled-sea contraction; at fixed
sigma,epsilon it converges to the prescribed continuum subtraction. For each
elementary bilinear the two subtractions differ by a vanishing scalar. After
the finite Wick expansion of the octic, the complete difference is therefore
a finite sum of lower-degree CAR words whose scalar coefficients vanish as
N->infinity; it is not in general one scalar multiple of the identity. At
sigma,epsilon>0 its kernel in the at most sixteen fermion variables is smooth.
Its tensor Fourier series is absolutely summable, so it is a norm-convergent
sum of bounded finite CAR words. The fixed-regulator QWZ limit follows
termwise from the elementary theorem, with the absolute Fourier sum providing
domination. More explicitly, if c_(r_1,...,r_s) are the tensor Fourier
coefficients of one fixed-regulator kernel, then

  sum_(r_1,...,r_s) |c_(r_1,...,r_s)| < infinity.         (19a)

For a Fourier basis vector e_r, the source estimate is S_0(e_r)=1, hence

  ||Psi_N(e_r)||=||R_N e_r||<=1

uniformly in N. Every corresponding CAR word therefore has norm at most one.
Equation (19a) is an N-independent Weierstrass majorant, so termwise QWZ
convergence passes through the infinite Fourier-word sum. Thus

  F8_(N,epsilon,sigma)(rho)V_N psi
    ->V_N F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)psi                      (20)

for psi in D_fin, and likewise for the adjoint.

It remains to remove sigma and epsilon in the continuum. Wick expansion of a
degree-eight polynomial in bilinear currents is finite. It has the form

  sum_lambda c_lambda
    :product_(l=1)^s_lambda partial^(q_lambda,l) psi^#_l:,

  s_lambda<=16,
  sum_l q_lambda,l+s_lambda/2=8,                           (21)

including lower fermion degrees generated by the prescribed contractions.
This is precisely why bosonic current normal ordering is not the naive
degree-sixteen fermion monomial.

The common-counterterm hypothesis used here is exact and limited. There is
one finite universal Wick polynomial W_(epsilon,sigma)[C] in the elementary
fields, the quasifree contraction C and its prescribed divided differences.
The microscopic expression evaluates it at the actual sea contraction C_N;
the continuum expression evaluates the same polynomial, with the same
coefficients and ordering, at C. At fixed sigma,epsilon all finitely many
mollified contractions and divided differences required by W converge from
C_N to C. Expanding the finite polynomial then gives the lower-degree
vanishing terms described above. If the two sides used independently chosen
subtraction constants rather than evaluations of this same Wick polynomial,
equation (20) would not follow. The retained current point-splitting
prescription supplies precisely this common W_(epsilon,sigma)[C]; it is a
premise of the local route, not a consequence of the energy estimate.

The subtraction terms must be combined into (21), or equivalently into
finite divided differences, before estimating them. For every fermion mode r,

  |exp(i r epsilon)|=1,
  |(exp(i r epsilon)-1)/epsilon|<=|r|,                     (22)

and the q-th divided difference is bounded by |r|^q. Mollifier multipliers
obey |etahat(sigma r)|<=1 and converge pointwise to one.

On P_E each fixed total field mode has only finitely many acting fermion-mode
tuples. Equations (21) and (22) give termwise convergence as sigma->0 and then
epsilon->0. They also give a polynomial majorant uniform in small sigma and
epsilon. A conservative count is

  number of tuples: O((1+E+|m|)^(s_lambda-1)),
  derivative weight: O((1+E+|m|)^(8-s_lambda/2)),

whose combined exponent is

  s_lambda/2+7 <=15.                                      (23)

Smooth rho absorbs the power fifteen just as in (11). Dominated summation over
m now yields, on every P_E,

  strong-lim_(epsilon->0) strong-lim_(sigma->0)
    F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)=F8(rho),                       (24)

and the same limit for the adjoint. The target in (24) is the mode-defined
F8(rho) of (12), because the retained point-splitting counterterms are exactly
the Wick contractions that produce (21); after taking matrix elements between
finite-energy vectors, both sides have the same finite mode coefficient at
every m. Combining (20) and (24) proves the fully ordered local route

  epsilon->0 after sigma->0 after N->infinity.             (25)

For compactly supported rho and eta, the fixed-regulator microscopic fields
are supported in the corresponding (sigma+|epsilon|)-neighborhoods of the
eight original preimage arcs. This is the correct local statement. No support
claim is made for the global finite-mode approximants of section 5, and no
uniform estimate allows the three limits in (25) to be collapsed into one
simultaneous limit.

If the old counterterms are left as separately divergent powers of epsilon
instead of first being combined into the Wick/divided-difference expression,
the bound (22) is unavailable and the local proof fails. Their algebraic
combination is therefore a load-bearing part of the theorem, not a notation
choice.

7. Energy-64 corollary and exact scope
--------------------------------------

The previous Ward result is the finite special case of the theorem. On the
continuum H<=64 space, the one-particle labels satisfy -63<=j<=64, and
Gamma(Pi_M) is the identity for N>=4096. The level-eight F8 state and
(Lhat_-1)^8 Omega_d therefore use a finite original CAR word inventory, and
their microscopic matrix element converges before any infinite mode sum is
needed.

ORIGINAL-CAR-WARD-IDENTIFICATION.txt now supplies the direct source-mode
calculation behind this corollary. With K=Lcal_-8/8 and

  c(r)=(r+alpha+4)/8,

the original CAR commutator and sea crossing give exactly

  [K,h_(k,-n)]=n h_(k,-n-1),
  K Omega=sum_k delta_k h_(k,-1) Omega.

Thus the finite energy-64 Ward vector is obtained inside the same original
CAR representation used above. Its exact nonzero amplitude is

  <F8;d|(Lcal_-8)^8 Omega>
    =sqrt(35)(16250979+56071008 i)/128.

The accompanying checker verifies the current QWZ strip atoms against the
preserved historical AST and checks 258 bounded source modes. Those finite
diagnostics corroborate this corollary; the all-energy smeared theorem rests
on equations (1)--(25), not on the finite diagnostic sample.

The theorem proves:

  * the same one-channel QWZ field, state, charge, time and adjoint feed the
    shifted neutral F8 smearing;
  * F8(rho) and its adjoint are closable energy-bounded continuum operators on
    the common finite-energy core;
  * both the global mode route and the separately mollified local route give
    transported strong iterated microscopic limits;
  * the exact energy-64 Ward amplitude is retained.

The theorem does not prove:

  * a bounded point field F8(x);
  * a simultaneous or regulator-independent diagonal N/UV limit;
  * exact interval support for finite current-mode cutoffs;
  * selection of the QWZ collar or quotient localization by P1/P2;
  * an odd E8 root/spinor field, full microscopic field exhaustion or a
    3+1-dimensional reconstruction.
