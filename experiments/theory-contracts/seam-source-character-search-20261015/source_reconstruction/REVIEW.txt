1. Compression identity and first missing source condition

For S=C U C on C H,

 C-S* S=C U* (1-C) U C=((1-C) U C)*((1-C) U C)

is exact. Therefore S is an isometry exactly when (1-C) U C=0. Fredholm
index -1 alone gives neither this leakage identity nor purity. Once isometry
is separately known, the index does give a one-dimensional wandering space.
The proof preserves this logical order and does not infer an operator from a
winding class.

2. Continuous scalar Hardy classification

The strengthening from rational to every continuous scalar unimodular symbol
is sound. Zero leakage implies M_u H2 is contained in H2; testing the constant
function shows that all negative Fourier coefficients of u vanish. For
continuous u, analytic Fejer sums converge uniformly, so u is the boundary
value of a disk-algebra function. Boundary modulus one gives a zero-free
collar, hence only finitely many interior zeros. Dividing their Blaschke
factors leaves a zero-free v in A(D), with 1/v also in A(D). Applying the
maximum principle to v and 1/v makes v a constant phase. The boundary winding
is consequently the number of zeros, so winding one gives exactly one
Blaschke factor.

This is exhaustive only for the stated continuous scalar Hardy-multiplier
class. The proof correctly does not transfer it to an unknown TFPT Calderon
pair or to general matrix-valued/bordism data.

The exact non-inner control is also correct:

 u_a=z^2/b_a,
 [z^(-m)]u_a=(1-a^2)a^(m+1),
 ||(1-C)u_a e_0||^2=a^4(1-a^2).

At a=1/4 the last value is 15/4096. Its phase derivative is 2-P_a and has
lower bound 2-(1+a)/(1-a), equal to 1/3 at a=1/4. Thus smoothness, strict
orientation and unit winding still do not imply zero leakage.

3. Purity, minimal dilation and time reconstruction

For one Blaschke factor, H2 minus b_a H2 is the span of the normalized kernel
k_a, and the intersection of b_a^n H2 over n>=0 is zero: membership forces a
zero of every order at a. This proves purity rather than merely fitting finite
modes.

Given leakage zero, index -1, purity and minimality, Wold decomposition yields

 C H = orthogonal_sum_(n>=0) U^n W,
 H   = orthogonal_sum_(n in Z) U^n W,

with dim W=1. The second equality follows from the stated minimal unitary
dilation hypothesis; it does not follow from the winding class alone.
Adding the actual common-core covariance U* B U=B+1 and B w=alpha w then gives
B U^n w=(n+alpha)U^n w for every integer n. The diagonal finite-span operator
is essentially selfadjoint, C is its nonnegative spectral projection for
0<alpha<1, and the quarter transition follows. These are correct conditional
consequences, and the proof labels both covariance and the wandering
eigenvalue as premises.

4. Bilateral Blaschke basis

The claimed basis k_a b_a^n, n in Z, is genuinely unitary, not a dimension
count. On the circle, if phi is the phase of b_a, then

 d phi/d theta=P_a=|k_a|^2.

Consequently the change of variables phi=arg b_a makes the Gram matrix of
k_a b_a^n the Fourier Gram matrix. This proves orthonormality. The associated
weighted composition operator is onto L2 because b_a is a circle
diffeomorphism; its restriction is the standard disk-automorphism unitary on
H2, so its nonnegative modes span H2. It therefore reduces H2 and commutes
with C. The conjugated B_a has the asserted covariance and quarter phase.
For a nonzero a it is correctly distinguished from the supplied
B_alpha=N+alpha, whose increment is P_a rather than one.
