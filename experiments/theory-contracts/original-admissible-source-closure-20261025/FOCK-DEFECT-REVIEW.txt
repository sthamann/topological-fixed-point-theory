1. Exact projection and commutator calculation
-----------------------------------------------

Since 0<a<1,

  C H=closed span{psi_n:n<0},
  P_+ H=closed span{psi_n:n>=0}.

The range of u C u* is the closed span of psi_m with m<=0. Hence

  u C u*=C+Pi,       C Pi=Pi C=0.                        (4)

Since P_+=I-C, the same identity is exactly the previously reconstructed
Toeplitz defect formula

  P_+-u P_+ u*=Pi.

Equivalently, on the complete mode basis,

  (u C-C u)psi_n = psi_0  if n=-1,
                    0      otherwise,

so that

  [u,C]=|psi_0><psi_(-1)|,
  rank[u,C]=1,
  ||[u,C]||_2=1.                                        (5)

The Shale-Stinespring criterion for CAR therefore applies: the one-particle
unitary u gives an implementable Bogoliubov automorphism in the pure
quasifree representation with covariance C. The implementation is projective,
so its only ambiguity here is an overall phase.

The reverse shift is genuinely different:

  u* C u=C-Pi_(-1),       Pi_(-1)=|psi_(-1)><psi_(-1)|,  (6)
  [u*,C]=-|psi_(-1)><psi_0|.

It removes one occupied negative mode instead of adding psi_0. Equations
(4)--(6) also fix the direction of every later charge and energy statement.

2. Fock convention, charge and the shifted vacuum
--------------------------------------------------

Use the CAR convention of GROUND-STATE-SOURCE-REDUCTION.txt. The filled-sea
vacuum obeys

  c_n Omega=0       for n>=0,
  c_n* Omega=0      for n<0.                            (7)

Let U satisfy (2), and put Xi=U Omega. From

  U* c_m U=c_(m-1)

one obtains

  c_m Xi=0          for m>=1,
  c_m* Xi=0         for m<=0.                           (8)

These are exactly the annihilation relations of the sea in which all n<0
modes and the additional n=0 mode are occupied. Since the quasifree Fock
vacuum for a fixed polarization is unique up to phase,

  Xi=phase c_0* Omega.                                  (9)

The norm is one because {c_0,c_0*}=1 and c_0 Omega=0.
Relative to the original sea, Xi has charge +1. With u* and the oppositely
oriented implementer, the state is the hole

  phase c_(-1) Omega,

of relative charge -1. This is the Fock counterpart of the minus sign in
(6).

The same result follows directly at the level of two-point functions. With

  omega_C(c(f)*c(g))=<g,Cf>,

the vector state of U Omega has

  <U Omega,c(f)*c(g)U Omega>
      =<g,u C u* f>
      =<g,Cf>+<g,Pi f>.                                 (10)

Thus Pi is an excess one-particle covariance. It is not the rank-one Fock
projection |Xi><Xi|; the two objects act on different Hilbert spaces.

3. Exact positive-energy line, with no heat limit
-------------------------------------------------

Assume the already stated source interface: the original positive-energy
Fock time restricts to

  alpha_t(c(f))=c(exp(i t B_a)f),
  alpha_t(A)=exp(i t H) A exp(-i t H),
  H>=0,       H Omega=0.                                (11)

This assumption is the existing full-CAR/time bridge. Under it the ground
state is forced to have C=P_-; no second quasifree state is chosen here.

Since B_a psi_0=a psi_0, anti-linearity of c and (11) give

  exp(i t H)c_0*Omega=exp(i t a)c_0*Omega,
  H c_0*Omega=a c_0*Omega.                              (12)

Consequently the Euclidean positive semigroup acts exactly by

  exp(-tH)c_0*Omega=exp(-ta)c_0*Omega.                  (13)

If

  Pi_F=|c_0*Omega><c_0*Omega|,

then

  Pi_F exp(-tH) Pi_F=exp(-ta)Pi_F.                      (14)

This is an exact reduced transition line, not an infrared or large-t heat
limit. For the reversed shift the hole c_(-1)Omega has energy

  -(a-1)=1-a.                                           (15)

Equations (12)--(15) use the Fock Hamiltonian H and its original time. They
do not identify the one-particle B_a with H, and they do not identify the
later electromagnetic response operator with Pi_F or Pi.

4. Relative Fredholm determinant and its orientation
-----------------------------------------------------

The ordinary determinants det(I-zC) and det(I-z(C+Pi)) are not defined as
Fredholm determinants: C has infinite rank, so the individual perturbations
of the identity are not trace class. Their directed quotient is well-defined.
For z different from 1, set

  A(z)=I-zC,
  A_u(z)=I-z(C+Pi).

Using C Pi=0 and Pi^2=Pi gives

  A(z) A_u(z)^(-1)
      =I+[z/(1-z)]Pi.                                   (16)

The right side differs from the identity by rank one. With the explicit
orientation

  det_rel(A,A_u):=det_F(A A_u^(-1)),

one obtains

  det_rel(A,A_u)=(1-z)^(-1).                            (17)

Reversing the ordered pair gives

  det_rel(A_u,A)=1-z.                                   (18)

For |z|<1 both are analytic at z=0, and (17)--(18) require no regularization
beyond the ordinary rank-one Fredholm determinant. At z=1 the inverse in
(16) is singular.

This exact factor is not by itself a CAR state sum. For one ordinary fermion
mode, the number-graded trace is

  Tr(z^N)=1+z,

while the supertrace is 1-z. A Berezin Gaussian with an inverse-kernel
deformation can also produce 1-z, with z acting as a source or kernel
coupling rather than a fugacity. The inverse factor in (17) is fixed by the
chosen direction of the relative covariance quotient. Therefore none of
(17), (18), 1+z or their powers may be substituted for another without the
original response, trace or Berezin contract. In particular, (17) does not
derive the later electromagnetic factor merely because it equals a familiar
rank-one expression.

5. Observable and superselection scope
---------------------------------------

If the physical Hilbert space is restricted to total charge zero, U Omega
lies in a different charge sector. A neutral observable cannot prepare it
from Omega. The seam implementer U is then a charged field or sector
intertwiner, not automatically an observable intervention.

The covariance difference remains visible to neutral observables. For every
f,g, the bilinear c(f)*c(g) is gauge invariant, and (10) gives the exact
neutral response

  omega_U(c(f)*c(g))-omega_C(c(f)*c(g))=<g,Pi f>.        (19)

Thus the rank-one defect survives as a gauge-invariant two-point response
even when the vector U Omega itself is not in the vacuum charge sector. This
does not show that the source permits U as a physical operation.
