INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE OLD DEFECT REWEIGHTING LEMMA
======================================================

25 October 2026. Narrow source and algebra review. No Atlas, index, paper or
archived source was changed. This note does not identify the reweighting
parameter with heat time, one-particle energy, charge or occupation number.

Verdict
-------

The old formula

  q(alpha)=q(0) exp(-alpha d s),                         (1)

is exact as a RELATIVE action statement if all of the following are part of
the sector contract:

  * the topological insertion d s is constant on the defect sector;
  * it is absent from the trivial sector;
  * after removing this insertion, the defect-to-trivial partition ratio is
    independent of alpha, including the measure and normalization factors.

The archived lemma states the first two ingredients in its intended
spectral-flow normalization, but its displayed hypotheses do not state the
third one. Indeed it writes both Z_triv(alpha) and Z_def(alpha), then infers
their ratio from an additive term in S_def alone. As written, that inference
is incomplete. It is valid if "defect sector reweighting" means that alpha
changes only the displayed relative topological action, or that every other
alpha dependence is common and cancels in the ratio.

The old choices

  d=2,       s=SF=1                                    (2)

are retained here as the existing original normalization contract. This
review neither derives nor rejects them and does not require identifying s
with energy, charge or a number operator. Its only question is what follows
from that contract for the complete sector ratio.

1. The exact complete-source identity
---------------------------------------

Let X_s and X_0 be the defect and trivial configuration spaces. After putting
both sectors over fixed reference descriptions, suppose

  S_s[x;alpha]=S_s^0[x;alpha]+alpha d s,                (3)
  S_0[x;alpha]=S_0^0[x;alpha],                          (4)

where d s is independent of x. Allow the residual measures to depend on
alpha. Define

  Z_s^0(alpha)=integral_(X_s) exp(-S_s^0[x;alpha])
                              d mu_(s,alpha)(x),
  Z_0^0(alpha)=integral_(X_0) exp(-S_0^0[x;alpha])
                              d mu_(0,alpha)(x),
  R(alpha)=Z_s^0(alpha)/Z_0^0(alpha).                   (5)

Then direct factorization gives

  Z_s(alpha)=exp(-alpha d s) Z_s^0(alpha),
  Z_0(alpha)=Z_0^0(alpha),

and therefore

  q(alpha):=Z_s(alpha)/Z_0(alpha)
           =exp(-alpha d s) R(alpha).                  (6)

At alpha=0 one has q(0)=R(0). Consequently the archived form (1) is exact on
an interval precisely when

  R(alpha)=R(0)                                         (7)

throughout that interval. If R is positive and differentiable, (7) is
equivalent to the relative Ward identity

  partial_alpha log R(alpha)=0                          (8)

throughout the interval. At only one value of alpha, equation (8) proves
first-order stationarity there, not global constancy.

Equivalently,

  partial_alpha log q(alpha)
     =-d s+partial_alpha log R(alpha).                  (9)

Thus -d s is the complete logarithmic slope exactly when the residual sector
ratio contributes no slope.

If the supposed insertion is instead a field-dependent functional J[x], the
factorization in (6) generally fails already within the defect sector:

  Z_s(alpha)=Z_s^0(alpha)
      <exp(-alpha J)>_(s,alpha;0).                      (10)

Its logarithm contains the cumulants of J. The old single exponential is
therefore exact only when the identified spectral-flow insertion is
sectorwise constant, or when an equivalent zero-fluctuation theorem is
provided. The intended identification J=d SF treats it as the constant
topological label and satisfies this requirement.

2. Fixed-measure Ward test
--------------------------

Suppose first that mu_s and mu_0 do not depend on alpha and differentiation
under both integrals is justified. Normalize expectations with the residual
weights exp(-S_s^0) and exp(-S_0^0). Then

  partial_alpha log Z_s^0
     =-<partial_alpha S_s^0>_s,
  partial_alpha log Z_0^0
     =-<partial_alpha S_0^0>_0.                         (11)

Hence

  partial_alpha log R
     =-<partial_alpha S_s^0>_s
      +<partial_alpha S_0^0>_0.                         (12)

The old reweighting follows exactly if and only if these two residual response
expectations agree for every alpha in the claimed interval. A sufficient
special case is that the residual actions are alpha independent. Another is
a common alpha deformation whose normalized expectations agree in the two
sectors. Neither conclusion follows merely from additivity of alpha d s in
the defect action.

3. Alpha-dependent measures
----------------------------

If the measures vary with alpha, first transport each sector to a fixed
reference space and write

  d mu_(r,alpha)=w_r(alpha,x) d mu_(r,*),       r=s,0.

Let

  sigma_r(alpha,x)=partial_alpha log w_r(alpha,x)       (13)

be the measure score. Under the usual differentiation and integrability
hypotheses,

  partial_alpha log R
    =-<partial_alpha S_s^0>_s+<sigma_s>_s
     +<partial_alpha S_0^0>_0-<sigma_0>_0.              (14)

Equation (14), rather than (12), is the complete Ward test. Moving domains,
gauge-fixing Jacobians and determinant normalizations must be included in the
scores after pullback. The archived exponential remains exact if the right
side of (14) vanishes. Measure compatibility alone means the ratio is
well-defined; cancellation still has to be stated or proved.

4. Smallest decisive countercheck of the omitted condition
-----------------------------------------------------------

Take one configuration in each sector, with unit counting measure, and set

  Z_s(alpha)=exp(-2 alpha),
  Z_0(alpha)=exp(-alpha).                               (15)

The defect action has the exact additive term 2 alpha. Nevertheless

  q(alpha)=Z_s(alpha)/Z_0(alpha)=exp(-alpha),            (16)

not exp(-2 alpha). In the notation above,

  d s=2,
  Z_s^0(alpha)=1,
  Z_0^0(alpha)=exp(-alpha),
  R(alpha)=exp(alpha).                                  (17)

Thus (6) gives exp(-2 alpha)exp(alpha)=exp(-alpha), as it
must. This is only an algebraic diagnostic of the missing hypothesis. It is
not a second P1 source, a physical countermodel or a refutation of the
intended relative normalization.

If instead both sectors carry a common baseline alpha and the defect sector
has the additional topological shift 2 alpha,

  Z_0(alpha)=exp(-alpha),
  Z_s(alpha)=exp(-3 alpha),                             (18)

then their ratio is exactly exp(-2 alpha). Equation (18) is the intended
relative-action situation in its smallest form.

5. Exact reading of the archived lemma
---------------------------------------

The relevant statements are:

  documents/archive--paper-latex--tfpt-theory-fullv27/sections/
  003-subsection-the-cubic-fixed-point-equation-cfe.tex:285-327

and

  documents/archive--paper-latex--tfpt-theory-fullv28/sections/
  004-subsection-geometric-self-consistency-backreaction.tex:17-59.

They define

  Z(alpha)=Z_triv(alpha)+Z_def(alpha),
  delta_top(alpha)=Z_def(alpha)/Z_triv(alpha),           (19)

state

  S_def(alpha)=S_def(0)+alpha J,                        (20)

and infer

  delta_top(alpha)=delta_top exp(-alpha J).             (21)

The texts then impose J=d J_1, J_1=SF, d=2 and SF=1. The cover-degree
statement supports the coefficient d once J is an integrated deck-invariant
constant response. It does not by itself freeze Z_triv(alpha) or the residual
determinant and measure ratio.

There are therefore two source-faithful readings:

  1. If (20) is intended to mean that the ONLY relative alpha deformation is
     the constant topological term, then (21) is exact. This is the natural
     intended reading of the words "defect sector reweighting". Equation (7)
     should be part of the hypothesis or of the definition of the normalized
     defect weight.

  2. If (19) denotes complete sector partition functions with otherwise
     unrestricted alpha-dependent actions or measures, the correct theorem is
     (6). Equation (21) then additionally requires the Ward identity (8), in
     the concrete form (12) or (14).

This is a missing hypothesis in the written lemma, not evidence that its
chosen spectral-flow normalization is wrong.

6. Relation to the recovered rank-one covariance determinant
-------------------------------------------------------------

FOCK-DEFECT-REVIEW.txt proves the directed algebraic identities

  det_F[(I-zC)(I-z(C+Pi))^(-1)]=(1-z)^(-1),
  det_F[(I-z(C+Pi))(I-zC)^(-1)]=1-z.                    (22)

Neither identity proves (8). For example, setting

  z=1-exp(-alpha d s)

makes the second factor in (22) equal exp(-alpha d s), but this is a parameter
substitution, not a derivation of the original action response. To use (22)
inside the old partition ratio, the original source must specify z as its
kernel or source deformation and show that every residual action, measure and
determinant contribution has the ratio R(alpha)=R(0). That last assertion is
exactly the Ward identity under review.

Conversely, the old reweighting argument does not need an identification of
alpha with heat time. If the same original source supplies the relative
action law (3), the constant d s and the cancellation (7), the exponential
follows immediately from its Euclidean sector weights. No equality with the
circle energy, charge, occupation number or positive heat semigroup is
required.

7. Scope and repaired statement
--------------------------------

The strongest statement supported by the displayed general hypotheses is:

  For measure-compatible sectors with a sectorwise constant topological
  insertion d s,

    q(alpha)=exp(-alpha d s) R(alpha).

  The original formula q(alpha)=q(0)exp(-alpha d s) holds exactly on a given
  interval if and only if the normalized residual sector ratio R is constant
  on that interval.

For the existing original contract d=2 and s=SF=1, this becomes

  q(alpha)=exp(-2 alpha)R(alpha),                        (23)

and reduces to the archived exp(-2 alpha) law when R(alpha)=R(0).

This review removes an unnecessary proposed heat-time identification. It
does not complete the fundamental source claim. That claim still requires
showing that (3), the sectorwise constant spectral-flow insertion and (7)
hold for the two sectors and measures of the SAME original source whose
response enters the electromagnetic closure.
