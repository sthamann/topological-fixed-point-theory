PAIR INDEX AND THE RELATIVE SEAM DETERMINANT
============================================

25 October 2026. Independent analytic review. No Atlas, index, paper or
existing contract was changed. This note reviews only the ordinary Fredholm
determinant and its precise TFPT scope.

Verdict
-------

The proposed theorem is correct after making its hypotheses and orientation
explicit.

Let P and Q be orthogonal projections on the same Hilbert space, assume
Q-P is trace class, and put

  k=Tr(Q-P).                                                (1)

Then k is an integer pair index with the sign convention in (1), and for
every |z|<1,

  det_F[(I-zQ)(I-zP)^(-1)] = (1-z)^k.                      (2)

The difference Q-P need not be positive or finite rank. In particular, a
primitive value k=1 fixes the scalar factor 1-z without selecting a primitive
line, a defect vector or a rank-one transfer operator.

If Q=UPU* for a unitary U satisfying Q-P trace class and if C is a positive
contraction with C-P trace class, the exact three-factor cancellation gives

  det_F[(I-z UCU*)(I-zC)^(-1)] = (1-z)^k.                  (3)

Thus the existing faithful KMS theorem extends from the old unit shift and its
integer powers to every unitary in the same restricted polarization class.
This is a genuine strengthening of the present contract. It weakens the need
for the specially named primitive ray only when the requested output is the
single scalar determinant response.

It does not reconstruct the primitive vector, transfer spectrum, state
preparation or the operator U_Sigma^min. It also does not derive the original
coefficient 5/4. An ordinary pair index is integral. The original formula uses
the already separate carrier weight B gamma=5/4 multiplying the logarithm.

1. Projection-pair proof and sign
---------------------------------

Write

  A_Q(z)=I-zQ,       A_P(z)=I-zP,
  R(z)=A_Q(z) A_P(z)^(-1).                                (4)

For |z|<1 both inverses exist. Moreover

  R(z)-I=-z(Q-P)A_P(z)^(-1)                               (5)

is trace class, so its ordinary Fredholm determinant is defined.

Direct differentiation gives

  R'(z)R(z)^(-1)
    =A_Q(z) [(P-Q)/(1-z)] A_Q(z)^(-1).                    (6)

For completeness, differentiate A_Q A_P^(-1), multiply on the right by
A_P A_Q^(-1), and use

  A_P^(-1)P=P/(1-z),       QA_Q^(-1)=Q/(1-z).

Since P-Q is trace class, cyclicity against the bounded invertible A_Q gives

  Tr[R'R^(-1)]
    =Tr(P-Q)/(1-z)
    =-k/(1-z).                                             (7)

The Fredholm Jacobi formula and det_F R(0)=1 now imply on the analytic branch
in the unit disk

  log det_F R(z)=k log(1-z),                               (8)

which is (2).

The sign is fixed by the stated order of the relative pair. On the actual old
negative sea

  P=1_(B_a<0),       u=M_z,       uPu*=P+Pi_0,

one has Q-P=Pi_0 and k=+1, hence 1-z. For the complementary positive Hardy
projection the same u gives k=-1 and the ordered factor is (1-z)^(-1).
Reversing U or reversing the ordered determinant also reverses k.

The integrality in (1) is the standard index theorem for a trace-class pair
of projections. With the Avron, Seiler and Simon convention
ind(P,Q)=Tr(P-Q), the present k equals -ind(P,Q). The bibliographic source is

  J. Avron, R. Seiler, B. Simon,
  The Index of a Pair of Projections,
  Journal of Functional Analysis 120 (1994), 220-237,
  DOI 10.1006/jfan.1994.1031.

The determinant identity itself follows from the short calculation above and
does not require a separate finite-rank reduction.

2. Exact extension from P to any trace-class covariance
--------------------------------------------------------

Assume now

  0 <= C <= I,       C-P in L1,
  Q=UPU*,            Q-P in L1.                            (9)

The phrase any U below always means a unitary satisfying the second condition
in (9). It does not mean every unitary on the Hilbert space.

Put

  A_C=I-zC,       A_UC=I-zUCU*.

Then UCU*-C is trace class because

  UCU*-C=U(C-P)U*+(Q-P)+(P-C).                            (10)

The relative operator has the exact factorization

  A_UC A_C^(-1)
   =[A_UC A_Q^(-1)]
    [A_Q A_P^(-1)]
    [A_P A_C^(-1)].                                      (11)

Every factor is identity plus trace class. The first factor is the unitary
conjugate of

  A_C A_P^(-1),

while the third factor is its inverse. Fredholm determinant multiplicativity
and unitary invariance cancel their determinants exactly. The middle factor is
the projection pair in (2). This proves (3) without commutativity of C with P
or U and without a sign assumption on UCU*-C.

Positivity of C is the natural covariance hypothesis and ensures the displayed
inverses for |z|<1. Algebraically, the same proof only needs bounded C and an
open z domain on which the inverses exist.

3. Smooth scalar winding on the actual circle
----------------------------------------------

Let P=P_- be the negative Fourier projection on L2(S1), and let U=M_u for a
smooth scalar function u:S1->U(1). Write

  u(theta)=sum_j u_j exp(i j theta).

For one Fourier monomial, [P,M_(z^j)] is a partial isometry of rank |j|.
Consequently

  ||[P,M_u]||_1 <= sum_j |j| |u_j|.                       (12)

The right side is finite for smooth u. Since

  UPU*-P=[U,P]U*,                                         (13)

the projection pair is trace class. The Toeplitz pair-index theorem, with the
negative-sea orientation used here, gives

  Tr(UPU*-P)=wind(u).                                      (14)

Equations (3) and (14) therefore yield

  det_F[(I-z UCU*)(I-zC)^(-1)]
    =(1-z)^(wind(u))                                      (15)

for every positive contraction C in the trace-class polarization class of P.
For u(theta)=exp(i theta), wind(u)=1 and (15) is the old factor. For the
positive Hardy projection the sign in (14) is reversed.

Smoothness is stronger than necessary. The operative sufficient condition in
(12) is the weighted Fourier summability sum |j||u_j|<infinity. Mere
boundedness or continuity of u does not by itself prove trace class.

4. Deduplizierung against the present Atlas and contract
--------------------------------------------------------

The current Atlas finding

  finding:old-seam-relative-determinant-is-stable-in-faithful-kms-class

already records the following narrower result:

  * C-P trace class gives 1-z for the actual old shift u;
  * the faithful C_beta=(I+exp(beta B_a))^(-1) belongs to that class;
  * integer powers u^m give (1-z)^m;
  * full carrier plus complement transport retains the same determinant.

The reviewed proofs are in

  GRADED-DETERMINANT-SOURCE.txt:435-566,
  KMS-AND-CARRIER-TRANSPORT.txt:38-159,
  RELATIVE-PAIRING-REVIEW.txt:411-475.

The new theorem does not repeat the faithful KMS calculation. Its additional
content is:

  * arbitrary trace-class projection pairs, including indefinite and
    infinite-rank differences;
  * arbitrary restricted unitaries U rather than only the translation subgroup
    generated by M_z;
  * the exact derivative (6)-(7);
  * the smooth scalar loop criterion and the full winding formula (15);
  * the conclusion that scalar determinant matching cannot select a ray.

The finite NS cyclic calculation in NS-CONTINUUM-WINDING-LIMIT.txt is an exact
control. There the finite projection pair has one infrared and one ultraviolet
crossing, hence Tr(Q_N-P_N)=0 and its ordinary determinant is one. Strong escape
of the ultraviolet vector does not change this finite pair index and does not
give trace-norm convergence. This agrees with (2); it is not an exception.

The reflected two-face pair is another control. Its two orientations have
indices k and -k, so their ordinary product determinant is one. Selecting one
labeled face is therefore still part of the directed original readout. The pair
index theorem does not create a chiral or sheet projection.

5. Susceptibility and multiparameter scope
------------------------------------------

For the ordinary determinant in (2), the complete scalar logarithmic response
is

  L(z)=log det_F R(z)=k log(1-z),
  L'(z)=-k/(1-z),
  L''(z)=-k/(1-z)^2.                                     (16)

Thus the projection geometry contributes only the locally constant integer k.
Smooth variation of U within one restricted-unitary component cannot generate
a continuously varying susceptibility: k stays constant until the
trace-class/Fredholm hypotheses fail or a different component is crossed.

If a physical parameter lambda enters only through a supplied scalar
z=z(lambda), then the theorem gives only the chain rule

  partial_a L=-k (partial_a z)/(1-z),

  partial_a partial_b L
   =-k[(partial_a partial_b z)/(1-z)
       +(partial_a z)(partial_b z)/(1-z)^2].               (17)

Equation (17) does not determine z(lambda), a metric on parameter space, or
independent cross susceptibilities. Several source variables, several
noncommuting insertions, or a matrix-valued fugacity require a new determinant
identity. They do not follow from the one-variable pair index.

6. The 5/4 carrier weight is not an ordinary index
---------------------------------------------------

An ordinary projection-pair determinant has the integer exponent k. Therefore

  k=5/4

is impossible in this theorem. The original v42 formula instead has the
well-typed structure

  W_Sigma(z)=-B gamma tr_adm log(1-z U_Sigma^min),
  B gamma=5/4.                                             (18)

If the scalar determinant supplied by the pair theorem has k=1, then retaining
the already declared carrier coefficient gives

  exp W_Sigma=(1-z)^(-5/4).                               (19)

This is a valid conditional compression of the scalar response. The pair index
supplies 1-z; it neither derives B gamma nor turns 5/4 into a topological index.

A usual graded determinant only replaces k by an integer signed index and does
not produce 5/4. A normalized or weighted trace could produce rational powers,
but it would be an additional functional. Its cyclicity, determinant
multiplicativity, branch and physical source would have to be specified and
proved. No such weighted determinant is needed for (19), because the original
already places B gamma outside tr_adm.

7. Exact TFPT impact
--------------------

For the scalar seam factor alone, the sufficient data are now smaller:

  * one original polarization P;
  * one positive covariance C with C-P trace class;
  * one supplied unitary U with UPU*-P trace class;
  * primitive oriented pair index Tr(UPU*-P)=1.

These data prove the base factor 1-z. Positivity, rank one and a distinguished
defect vector are unnecessary. Therefore a proof which uses only the scalar
factor must not claim that this factor has reconstructed psi_Sigma or selected
U_Sigma^min.

The special ray remains necessary wherever the theory asks for more than that
scalar, for example a primitive state, a finite transfer spectrum, matrix
elements, repeated interventions, a carrier embedding or a prepared physical
mode. Different restricted unitaries with the same pair index give the same
determinant and may have different operator defects. Equation (2) deliberately
forgets those differences.

The smallest decisive original test is consequently no longer a rank-one test.
It is the oriented pair-index test on the actual supplied boundary operation:

  C_Sigma-P in L1,
  U_Sigma P U_Sigma*-P in L1,
  Tr(U_Sigma P U_Sigma*-P)=1.                              (20)

If (20) holds, the scalar determinant portion of the seam response is closed
without selecting a ray. The separate source law z=q(alpha), the carrier
coefficient B gamma=5/4 and every nonscalar physical readout retain their own
existing provenance requirements. This is the precise gain of the theorem,
not a new full electromagnetic or TFPT closure.

Independent checks
------------------

A finite-dimensional numerical check used noncommuting projection pairs with
rank differences 2, 0 and -3 and two complex values of z. It verified (2),
the full operator equality (6) and trace sign (7) to below 1e-10. A separate
positive contraction and noncommuting unitary check verified the three-factor
cancellation at pair index zero. These calculations are diagnostics; the proof
above is analytic.
