  z^8 Y_d(P,z)|d> = sum_(ell>=0) v_ell z^ell

  h_j(-n) -> (-1)^(n-1)d_j
             + sum_(r>=n) binom(r-1,n-1) h_j(-r) z^r.       (1)

Equation (1) follows directly from

  Y(h_j(-n),z)=partial^(n-1)h_j(z)/(n-1)!

preserved.  The checker expands (1) sparsely through level eight and uses the
positive Heisenberg norm

  ||product_(r,j) h_j(-r)^m(r,j)|d>||^2
    = product_(r,j) r^m(r,j) m(r,j)! .                      (2)

Why nine coefficients determine the entire separated function
---------------------------------------------------------------

Let q_rad=w_coordinate/z_coordinate with |q_rad|<1 and set

  F_d(q_rad)=z^8 w_coordinate^8 <d|W8(z)W8(w_coordinate)|d>.

The state expansion and radial adjoint give

  F_d(q_rad)=sum_(ell>=0) a_ell q_rad^ell,
  a_ell=||v_ell||^2.                                      (8)

The field is bosonic and mutually local, so its separated analytic
continuation obeys F_d(q_rad)=F_d(1/q_rad).  After the displayed powers of z
and w are removed, Wick contractions have only the collision pole q_rad=1.  Its order is at
most 16 because W8 has weight eight.  The source terms d/z add no new separated
pole.

Every q_rad<->1/q_rad invariant rational function is rational in
q_rad+q_rad^-1.  Since

  q_rad+q_rad^-1-2 = (1-q_rad)^2/q_rad,

finiteness at q_rad=0 and the absence of any pole except q_rad=1 imply that F_d is a
polynomial in

  X = q_rad/(1-q_rad)^2.

The collision bound fixes its degree to at most eight:

  F_d(q_rad)=sum_(r=0)^8 A_r X^r.                          (9)

For 1<=ell<=8, comparison of (8) and (9) is triangular:

  a_ell=sum_(r=1)^ell binom(ell+r-1,2r-1) A_r,
  a_0=A_0.                                                 (10)

Thus the nine exact Fock norms a_0,...,a_8 determine the entire separated
function.  Equation (9) is a consequence of locality, exchange symmetry, and
the pole bound, rather than a fit to nine samples.  All higher creation modes
are thereby retained in the resulting rational function.

Why the two quarters agree
--------------------------

Let R reverse the eight original source coordinates.  The exact matrix

  g=-M R M^-1

acts in standard coordinates as

  g(x0,x1,x2,x3,x4,x5,x6,x7)
    =(x3,-x4,-x5,x0,-x1,-x2,x7,x6).
