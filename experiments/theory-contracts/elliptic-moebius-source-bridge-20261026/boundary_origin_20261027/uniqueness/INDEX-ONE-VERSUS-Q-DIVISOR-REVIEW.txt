INDEPENDENT REVIEW: INDEX ONE VERSUS THE THREE-POINT q DIVISOR
2026-10-27

VERDICT

The rank obstruction is correct for a claimed invertible Fredholm
identification of the old one-mode seam defect with the full D_q evaluation
defect.  The full defect changes the index by -3, while the old directed
Fourier seam shift changes it by -1.  An invertible Fredholm intertwiner
cannot identify those two operators.

There is a precise one-dimensional escape: at a c-linearized fiber, the
three evaluation fibers form the regular C3 representation and its invariant
character line has dimension one.  Imposing only that character condition
changes the index by -1.  This removes the rank obstruction, but it does not
produce the already required inverse norm line.  Its directed determinant
has the opposite beta phase.  Thus the invariant-character route is allowed
as a new typed test, not yet an identification with N_q^(-1).


1. THE FULL DOMAIN DEFECT HAS INDEX MINUS THREE

Let

  E=S_0 tensor L,
  D_q=q_0+q_1+q_2.

For genus two, deg(E)=1 and therefore

  chi(E)=deg(E)+1-g=0.

After imposing vanishing at all three q points,

  deg(E(-D_q))=-2,
  chi(E(-D_q))=-3.                                             (1)

The evaluation sequence

  0 -> E(-D_q) -> E -> E|D_q -> 0                             (2)

accordingly changes the Fredholm index by -3.  Equivalently,

  lambda(E(-D_q)) tensor lambda(E)^(-1)
    =det H^0(D_q,E|D_q)^(-1)                                  (3)

is the inverse determinant of a rank-three quotient.

If the recovered Fourier seam operation is a directed index-minus-one
Fredholm defect, no bounded invertible intertwiner can turn it into (2):
Fredholm index is invariant under such an intertwining.  This conclusion is
limited to an asserted identity of the two full operators.  It does not
exclude a projection, an equivariant summand, a noninvertible pushforward, or
additional kernel and cokernel data.


2. THE CUBIC CHARACTER DECOMPOSITION CONTAINS AN INDEX-ONE SUMMAND

At a fiber carrying a c linearization, c cyclically permutes the three q
fibers.  Hence

  H^0(D_q,E|D_q)=F_1 direct_sum F_omega direct_sum F_omega2,   (4)

with each character line one-dimensional.  Composing evaluation with the
projection to F_1 and requiring only this invariant component to vanish gives
a codimension-one domain condition.  Its index change is -1.

This construction is not available fiberwise for generic L in Jac(C).
Generally c sends E_L to E_(cL), rather than acting on one fixed E_L.  A
single-fiber invariant line requires a c-fixed L together with a
linearization, or an explicitly constructed equivariant family over a c
orbit.  The source-selected L=O fiber satisfies this requirement, but it
does not justify the same reduction for the generic theta family.

The nontrivial character lines are exchanged by Q because Q conjugates c to
c^(-1).  The invariant line is the only one-dimensional character summand
stable under that relation.  Thus it is the correct smallest equivariant
rank test, but equivariance alone does not determine its determinant
orientation.


3. THE BETA PHASE EXCLUDES THE NAIVE IDENTIFICATION

On the marked q fiber, beta acts by +i on every evaluation fiber.  It
therefore acts as follows:

  beta|F_1=+i,
  beta|F_1^(-1)=-i,                                           (5)

whereas on the full permutation-free norm,

  beta|N_q=i^3=-i,
  beta|N_q^(-1)=+i.                                           (6)

The directed index-minus-one relative determinant obtained by imposing the
invariant condition is F_1^(-1), by the same convention as (3).  Its beta
phase is -i, opposite to the +i phase of N_q^(-1).  In spin notation, its
alpha square is likewise -i rather than +i.

The undualized invariant quotient F_1 has the desired +i scalar, but it is
the opposite determinant orientation.  Identifying it with the old directed
defect would reverse the relative line or the index convention.  Such a
reversal must come from the original operator orientation; it cannot be made
solely to match the phase.

The full norm product succeeds because it multiplies all three character
lines before dualizing:

  det(F_1 direct_sum F_omega direct_sum F_omega2)^(-1)
    has phase (i^3)^(-1)=+i.                                  (7)

One character line and the product of all three are therefore not
interchangeable, even though both can be one-dimensional determinant lines.


4. EXACT SCOPE

The test proves:

  * the old one-mode index-minus-one shift is not invertibly identical to the
    full three-point D_q domain restriction;

  * a c-invariant rank-one restriction is possible at a c-linearized fiber;

  * its naturally directed inverse determinant has the wrong beta and alpha
    square for N_q^(-1);

  * the full product of three character lines has the correct phase but the
    index-minus-three domain defect.

It does not prove that no original boundary construction can produce
N_q^(-1).  A valid construction may retain the full three-point determinant,
use a noninvertible index pushforward, or contain a separately justified
orientation or duality.  What is excluded is the simple claim that the old
single Fourier defect is already the full q evaluation defect, or that its
c-invariant part is automatically the inverse q norm.

The next required identity remains operator-typed: the original source must
specify which evaluation character or full determinant enters, with which
relative orientation, on a c and Q compatible domain.  No phase-only
identification supplies that information.
