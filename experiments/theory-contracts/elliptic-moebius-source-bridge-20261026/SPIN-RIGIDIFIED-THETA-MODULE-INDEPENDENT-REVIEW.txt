1. THE FOURTH-ROOT SET IS A GROUP OF ORDER 64

The theta group G(O(2Theta)) is a class-two central extension of A[2] by C*.
Its commutator on two lifts is the Weil pairing and therefore lies in
{+1,-1}.  For g^4=h^4=1,

  (gh)^4=g^4 h^4 [h,g]^6=1.

The convention for the order of the commutator can invert the last factor,
but its sixth power is one in either convention.  Inverses retain fourth
power one, so

  N={g:g^4=1}

is a subgroup.  Above each of the sixteen points of A[2], a theta lift can be
rescaled by exactly four scalars to have fourth power one.  Thus |N|=64 and
its kernel is mu4.  Nondegeneracy of the Weil pairing forces every central
element to have zero image in A[2], hence Z(N)=mu4.  This is the standard
two-qubit finite Heisenberg group, defined intrinsically inside the theta
group.

2. SYMPLECTIC LIFTS AND STONE-VON NEUMANN

For a symplectic basis of A[2], multiplying a chosen lift by i reverses its
square.  The four basis lifts can therefore be normalized to square +1, with
the two standard commutators equal to -1 and all other basis commutators
trivial.  The resulting presentation is the Pauli presentation with center
mu4.  Every specified symplectic Phi consequently lifts to a
center-preserving isomorphism N->N64.

The lift is not canonical.  Two lifts differ by a character A[2]->mu2, and
nondegeneracy of the commutator identifies every such character with
conjugation by a Pauli element.  This is exactly the Heisenberg-frame freedom
retained in the report.

Riemann-Roch gives dim H^0(O(2Theta))=4.  Its theta-group representation has
central weight one.  Finite Stone-von Neumann gives one irreducible
representation with this faithful central character, of dimension four.
The constant line N_q(S_0)^(-1) changes the alpha and beta characters but not

Conjugation by a Pauli q changes p by (1-Sigma)q, up to a central scalar that
is absorbed in lambda.  The cubic action is fixed-free on the Pauli quotient,

The remaining scalar is also fixed.  Gamma has order three and is conjugate

  tr(Ad gamma)=1=|tr gamma|^2.

trace one.  Thus the scalar in lambda sigma is lambda=1.  The argument uses
