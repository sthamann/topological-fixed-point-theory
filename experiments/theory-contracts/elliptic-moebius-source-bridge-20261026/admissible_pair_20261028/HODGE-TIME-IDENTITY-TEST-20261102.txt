HODGE CHANNEL AND A CONTINUOUS IDENTITY START
============================================

2 November 2026. Exact finite-dimensional consequence of the given Hodge
amplitudes. This is not an identification of TFPT physical time, a deduction
of an original measurement, or a no-go for a larger process with additional
amplitudes, registers, memory, or a distinguished finite-time event.

Let H=Lambda^2 C^5, dim H=10, and C_i:H -> H* be the five original epsilon
amplitudes from hodge_complement_channel.py. An arbitrary unitary identification
H* -> H may be made before comparing them with an identity evolution. Since the
result below is uniform over all unitary target maps, that identification does
not affect the bound.

Write |A>> for vectorization, <A|B>>=tr(A*B), and

 P5 = (1/6) sum_i |C_i>><<C_i|,
 J(Phi) = (1/3) sum_i |C_i>><<C_i| = 2 P5,
 tr J(Phi)=10.

The original amplitudes satisfy tr(C_i* C_j)=6 delta_ij. For EVERY complex
coefficient vector z, C(z)=sum_i z_i C_i obeys

 (C(z)*C(z))^2 = ||z||^2 C(z)*C(z),
 tr(C(z)*C(z)) = 6 ||z||^2.

The checker verifies both identities as polynomials for all five complex
coefficients, with the conjugate variables independent until the end. Thus for
z != 0 precisely six singular values equal ||z||, and the remaining four are
zero. For any unitary U:H -> H*, Holder's inequality gives

 |tr(C(z)*U)| <= ||C(z)||_1 = 6||z||,
 <<U|P5|U>> = (1/6) sum_i |tr(C_i*U)|^2 <= 6.              (1)

The second line also follows by maximizing over the unit coefficient vector
z. The bound is sharp: the checker extends the actual signed partial
permutation C_0 to a 10 by 10 unitary, attaining 6. Set omega_U=|U>><<U|/10
and omega_Phi=J(Phi)/10. They are the normalized Choi output states obtained
from the same maximally entangled reference input. The effect P5 gives

 tr(P5 omega_Phi)=1,   tr(P5 omega_U)<=3/5.

For any two states, their trace distance is at least the absolute difference
of the probabilities for an effect. The diamond norm includes this single
reference-assisted input, hence

 ||Phi - Ad_U||_diamond >= 2(1 - 3/5) = 4/5                 (2)

for every U. In fact the exact normalized Choi trace distance sharpens this.
Let u=|U>>/sqrt(10), q=<u|P5|u> <= 3/5. There are four eigenvalues 1/5
on P5 perpendicular to its projection of u; the two remaining eigenvalues
have opposite sign for q<1 and give

 ||P5/5 - |u><u|||_1 = 4/5 + sqrt(36/25 - 4q/5)
                       >= (4+2 sqrt(6))/5 = 1.779795897... .  (3)

The unitary completing C0 has q=3/5, so the MINIMUM OF THIS CHOI lower bound
over unitaries is attained. The actual diamond distance is nevertheless 2,
and this holds FOR EVERY U, by the following separate argument.

For fixed U, let K be the set of complex five-vectors with coordinates
Tr(rho U* C_i), where rho runs over all density operators on H. K is a
compact convex subset of C^5. If zero were not in K, strict convex separation
would give a complex vector z such that Re Tr(rho U* C(z))>0 for every rho.
This would mean Re(U* C(z)) is strictly positive definite. However, C(z) has
a nonzero kernel for every z, so for a nonzero x in that kernel,
<x,Re(U* C(z))x>=0. Contradiction. Thus some density rho obeys
Tr(rho U* C_i)=0 simultaneously for all i.

Here C^5 is treated as R^10: every real linear functional can be expressed as
Re sum_i z_i a_i after relabelling the arbitrary complex coefficients z_i.
Hence no unstated complex linear separation or conjugation convention is used.

Purify rho=sum_j p_j |e_j><e_j| as the unit vector
|Omega_rho>=sum_j sqrt(p_j)|e_j> tensor |j> on an untouched reference.
It has norm one, and for any A on the carrier,
<Omega_rho|(A tensor I)|Omega_rho>=Tr(rho A). The inner product of the output for U
and each Hodge Kraus output is precisely Tr(rho U* C_i)/sqrt(3)=0. Hence the
pure unitary output is orthogonal to the support of the Hodge output, and the
states are perfectly distinguishable in one use. Both output states have
trace one because U and Phi are trace preserving. Consequently

 ||Phi - Ad_U||_diamond = 2   for every U:H -> H*.           (4)

This convex argument is not an original general distinguishability theorem;
compare the operator-subspace criterion for channel discrimination in the
primary papers https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02294 and
https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.0119. Its present input is the specific rank-six
identity of the original epsilon amplitudes.

Explicitly, in the original ordered duad basis (01,02,03,04,12,13,14,23,
24,34), U equals C0 on the last six basis states and is identity on the
first four. On the input |01>, U outputs |01>, while C0|01>=C1|01>=0,
C2|01>=|34>, C3|01>=-|24>, C4|01>=|23>. The Hodge output is therefore
(|34><34|+|24><24|+|23><23|)/3, orthogonal to |01><01|. This is an
unassisted perfect distinguisher and proves distance 2 for that actual U.

Combined with the already established R086 shared-TP theorem, any complete
globally TP operation whose every joint Kraus operator lies strictly in
span{C_i} tensor B(F,F') has precisely Phi as carrier marginal, including on
correlated inputs. Consequently such a family cannot be a finite-dimensional
continuous evolution with a unitary/identity carrier limit at t=0 for all
sufficiently small t. Finite-dimensional strong continuity gives the needed
channel norm continuity. This remains true for any unitary choice of the
carrier/dual identification, even if that choice varies with t.

An explicit compatible mathematical embedding
---------------------------------------------

Fix any unitary U:H -> H*, and write Psi=Ad_(U*) o Phi, now a CPTP map on H.
For any separately chosen lambda>0 the bounded generator L=lambda(Psi-id)
defines the continuous CPTP semigroup

 E_t = exp(t L) = exp(-lambda t) sum_(k>=0) (lambda t)^k Psi^k/k!,
 E_t = id + lambda t(Psi-id) + O(t^2).

Every term is CPTP and the nonnegative Poisson weights sum to one. The k=0
Kraus operator exp(-lambda t/2)I is the unchanged carrier branch; the
other branches concatenate the actual original epsilon Kraus operators
U*C_i/sqrt(3). The checker verifies trace preservation for the one- and
two-jump terms. The jump intensity lambda, the Poisson schedule and the
identification U are mathematical choices here, not outputs of P1, S_adm,
the TFPT clock network or a physical time scale.

Each nonzero C(z) has rank six, whereas U has rank ten. Thus U is outside
span{C_i}; equivalently I is outside span{U*C_i}. Further, this is not merely
an artifact of the displayed Kraus decomposition. If any Kraus decomposition
of E_t were entirely in that strict span, the original 25-product TP theorem
would force E_t=Psi. But E_t -> id as t -> 0, while (3) bounds Psi away from
id (indeed (4) gives distance 2). Moreover
||E_t-id||_diamond <= 2(1-exp(-lambda t)) < 2 for EVERY finite t, so E_t
cannot equal Psi at ANY finite time. Hence at least one Kraus amplitude must
be outside the strict span for EVERY Kraus decomposition of E_t at EVERY
finite t. This conclusion says nothing about which extra amplitudes are
actually supplied by the original source or about the infinite-time limit.

This is an actual compatibility condition on how the previously identified
E8 bracket operation can enter a source/time dictionary: it can be a
particular event or finite transition; alternatively, a full continuous
process needs other components, such as an identity/no-event amplitude,
outside this strict bracket-only Kraus span. We do not infer which of these
the original source chooses. A single sub-TP branch need not obey R086's
global-TP conclusion. Original reflection, two boundaries, dynamics, family,
Eta, reference, and the measured or discarded register must still be mapped.
