2. Direct algebraic check

Take configurations (x,y) in {+a,-a}^2, reflection theta(x,y)=(y,x),
 P=[[0,1],[1,0]], D_xy=[[x,g],[g,y]], a>g>0.
Then P D_xy P=D_yx exactly. For x=y the squared eigenvalues are
(a-g)^2,(a+g)^2. For x=-y both squared eigenvalues are a^2+g^2.
All D_xy are invertible, with minimum absolute eigenvalue at least a-g.

The base configuration weight/RP matrix K0=[[10,9],[9,10]] has eigenvalues
19 and 1, so it is strictly RP; all entries are strictly positive as well.
It may be normalized by its positive total mass without affecting RP.

For f(t)=exp(-t), a=2,g=1, write
 S_same=exp(-1)+exp(-9), S_opp=2exp(-5),
 Delta=S_same-S_opp=exp(-1)(1-exp(-4))^2>0.
All four Dirac blocks have gap at least 1. The spectral weight is positive
and reflection-invariant. Its reweighted RP matrix is
 K=[[10exp(-S_same),9exp(-S_opp)],
    [9exp(-S_opp),10exp(-S_same)]].
The half-observable F(x)=sign(x) has reflected unnormalized square
 F* K F=2[10exp(-S_same)-9exp(-S_opp)].
This is strictly negative iff Delta>log(10/9).

The proposed strict rational estimate is correct. Put z=exp(-1), so
1/3<z<1/2. Then
 Delta=z(1-z^4)^2 > (1/3)(15/16)^2=75/256
       >1/9>log(10/9).
The last inequality is log(1+u)<u for u>0. Thus the normalized reflected
expectation is also strictly negative; the normalization constant is positive.
There is no numerical tolerance, spectral crossing or zero-mode issue.

3. General profile calculation

For the source's positive Laplace representation,
 Delta=2 integral exp[-s(a^2+g^2)] [cosh(2sag)-1] dnu(s).
The integral is strictly positive whenever nu gives positive mass to s>0,
equivalently whenever this finite-measure Laplace profile is nonconstant.
A constant part at s=0 cancels. All terms are finite for a>g>0.

For an arbitrary such profile the specific base ratio 9/10 need not violate
RP: its violation still requires Delta>log(10/9). The general invalidity of
the implication nevertheless persists for every nonconstant profile: choose
 K0=[[1,r],[r,1]], exp(-Delta)<r<1.
This base kernel is strictly RP and entry-positive, while its spectral
reweighting has a negative sign-vector eigenvalue. This does not claim that
the actual TFPT reference measure can be freely changed to that kernel.

4. Positive repair criterion

For a fixed classical split reference measure, the exact required statement
is positivity of the fully weighted reflected kernel. A useful stronger
condition is that the weight itself be a positive-definite kernel across
the reflection, or belong to the closed RP weight cone
 w=sum_k c_k theta(B_k) B_k, c_k>=0,
with B_k supported on the positive half and limits justified in a topology
that preserves the relevant integrals. For an RP reference measure,
 integral theta(F) F w dmu
   =sum_k c_k integral theta(B_k F) (B_k F) dmu >=0.
For a genuine positive reweighted measure also retain w>=0 and a finite
positive normalizer. In finite split spaces the same sufficient statement
is the Schur product theorem: K0>=0 and weight matrix W>=0 imply
K0 entrywise-times W>=0. Entrywise positivity of W is insufficient.

Factorization w=theta(B)B is a simple special case; a reflection-splitting
action has this form after exponentiation. The actual nonlocal spectral
trace requires its own such factorization, cone membership, or a direct
weighted RP proof. The example's W has same-diagonal value exp(-S_same)
smaller than its off-diagonal value exp(-S_opp), so W is not positive
semidefinite and the proposed preservation inference cannot be used.

Primary comparison checked online: Jobst Ziebell, theta-splitting densities
and reflection positivity, Letters in Mathematical Physics 114,53 (2024),
Definition3.4 and Theorem3.5; Lemma2.4 gives the Schur product argument.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11005-024-01799-8
Its theorem uses an explicitly theta-splitting density and its stated
Gaussian reference assumptions. It is not a general positive-weight theorem.
The elementary finite split proof above is given separately and does not
import a Gaussian hypothesis into this counterexample.
