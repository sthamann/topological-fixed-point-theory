2. Exact finite reflected-measure test

There are two halves, each carrying a sign s,t in {-1,+1}. Reflection exchanges
them and conjugates scalars. The positive-half algebra is all functions F(t).
An unnormalized symmetric joint measure is the matrix K_st. Its reflected form
is sum_st conjugate(F(s)) K_st F(t), divided by sum_st K_st for normalization.
Thus reflection positivity is exactly K positive semidefinite.

Use the base kernel K0=[[10,9],[9,10]]. All entries are positive, both eigenvalues
are positive (19,1), and the associated normalized transfer is primitive. For
F(t)=t, the original normalized reflected form is 1/19>0. This is a strictly RP
base; the example does not exploit a null vector of a merely semidefinite base.

For a>g>0 define the self-adjoint matrix
  D(s,t)=[[a s,g],[g,a t]].
With R=[[0,1],[1,0]] it obeys R D(s,t) R=D(t,s). Its squared spectra are
  s=t: (a-g)^2,(a+g)^2;
  s=-t: a^2+g^2,a^2+g^2.
At a=2,g=1 every matrix is invertible with absolute spectral gap at least1.
This is a finite spectral test, not an elliptic geometric/BRST realization.

Take f(u)=exp(-u), a positive completely monotone heat profile with positive
Laplace measure delta_1, in the class called the Bernstein cone by the source.
It is not a Bernstein function in the separate conventional increasing sense.
For S(s,t)=Tr f(D(s,t)^2), q=exp(-1), one gets
  S_same=q+q^9,
  S_opposite=2q^5,
  Delta=S_same-S_opposite=q(1-q^4)^2>0.
The Boltzmann weights w(s,t)=exp[-S(s,t)] are strictly positive and invariant
under the reflection. The weighted reflected kernel is nonetheless
  K=[[10 exp(-S_same),9 exp(-S_opposite)],
     [9 exp(-S_opposite),10 exp(-S_same)]].
Its eigenvalue in the F(t)=t direction is
  lambda_minus=exp(-S_opposite)[10 exp(-Delta)-9].

This sign is rigorously negative. The elementary series for e gives 2<e<3,
so 1/3<q<1/2 and
  Delta>(1/3)(15/16)^2=75/256>1/9>log(10/9).
Consequently 10 exp(-Delta)<9. Normalization by a positive partition function
cannot change that sign. Explicitly,

    =[10 exp(-S_same)-9 exp(-S_opposite)]
      /[10 exp(-S_same)+9 exp(-S_opposite)]
    =-0.1239426461250916662535013947009952773381...
The number is a value of THIS counterexample, not a computed TFPT response.
Every configuration has positive ordinary probability, but the reflected form

4. The exact sufficient repair

In a finite classical split, let W_st=w(s,t). Entrywise reweighting is
  K=K0 elementwise-times W.
If W is positive semidefinite, the Schur product theorem gives K>=0 for every
K0>=0. Conversely, a multiplier preserving every such K0 must have W>=0 by
testing K0=all-ones; strict positive bases follow by small diagonal perturbation.
Thus pointwise positive entries of W must not be substituted for W>=0.
For a particular fixed base, K0 elementwise-times W>=0 is the exact criterion;
W>=0 alone is sufficient, not necessary for that particular base.

For bounded functions on a classical reflected configuration space, the
factorization
  w=sum_j c_j Theta(B_j) B_j,  c_j>=0, B_j on the positive half,
gives q_w(F)=sum_j c_j q_0(B_j F)>=0. Controlled integrable limits preserve the
same conclusion. A factorization exp[-S_plus-Theta(S_plus)] is a special case.
Odd fermionic products require their actual graded/twisted conventions; the
commutative proof is not silently extended to arbitrary Grassmann densities.

For the actual P1 claim, one must derive such a suitable sewing structure from
