1. The actual quotient and its generators
===========================================

For

  X=P1 minus mu4

the D4 invariant quotient function is

  J(x)=1/2-(x^4+x^(-4))/4
      =-(x^4-1)^2/(4x^4).

The points x in mu4 are removed and lie over J=0 with ramification index two.
The four points x^4=-1 lie over J=1 with index two, while zero and infinity
lie over J=infinity with index four. Thus the quotient orbifold has type

  (infinity,2,4).

Choose its positively oriented generators with

  a b c=I,       b^2=I,       c^4=I.

The pullback peripheral at each original puncture is

  M=a^2.                                                 (1)

The marked order four fiber operator is fixed as

  c=diag(1,i,-i),                                       (2)

the determinant one normalization of the original homological Coxeter
rotation. Equations (1) and (2) concern a representation of the quotient
orbifold fundamental group. Its image need not be the finite geometric D4
group. This prevents the common quotient versus fiber action confusion.

2. Classification of the involution b
======================================

Assume the representation is unitary with determinant one and M has the
regular cubic spectrum

  spec(M)={1,omega,omega^2}.

The choice b=I would give a=c^(-1) and

  a^2=c^(-2)=diag(1,-1,-1),

which is not regular cubic. Hence b is a nontrivial involution in SU(3).
It is Hermitian and has eigenvalues (1,-1,-1), so uniquely

  b=2 v v^*-I                                         (3)

for a unit vector v. Write

  p_j=|v_j|^2,       p_1+p_2+p_3=1.

Since a=(bc)^(-1), unitarity gives

  tr(bc)=conjugate(tr(a)).                              (4)

Directly from (2) and (3),

  tr(bc)=2[p_1+i(p_2-p_3)]-1.                         (5)

No phase of v enters (5).

3. The four square root branches
=================================

Because a^2=M is regular cubic and det(a)=1, the possible eigenvalue sets of
a give exactly

  tr(a) in {0,2,2 omega,2 omega^2}.                    (6)

This can be obtained by choosing determinant one square roots of
{1,omega,omega^2}; there are four sign branches.

If tr(a)=2, equations (4) and (5) require

  p_1=3/2,

which is impossible.

If tr(a)=2 omega or 2 omega^2, they require

  p_1=0.

Then v is orthogonal to the c eigenvalue one line Ce_1, so b e_1=-e_1.
Both b and c preserve Ce_1. Consequently a and M=a^2 preserve it as well,
and the source pair <c,M> is reducible. Stability or irreducibility excludes
both branches.

The only remaining branch has tr(a)=0. Equations (4) and (5) now force

  p_1=1/2,       p_2=p_3=1/4.                          (7)

In this branch the eigenvalues of a are the three cube roots of unity, so

  a^3=I.                                                (8)

Thus the finite triangle relation is derived from unitary descent, the
regular local class, and irreducibility. It is not inserted as a modular
Level 4 assumption.

4. Uniqueness and exact v117 identification
============================================

The phases of a vector satisfying (7) are removed by a diagonal unitary in
the centralizer of c. A common scalar on that diagonal does not alter the
conjugation and can, if desired, make its determinant one. Therefore every
irreducible solution is centralizer conjugate to

  v_0=(1/sqrt(2),1/2,1/2)^T,
  b_0=2 v_0 v_0^*-I,
  a_0=(b_0 c)^(-1).                                    (9)

Direct multiplication gives b_0^2=I, a_0^3=I and a_0^2 regular cubic. With

  D=diag(1,-(1+i)/sqrt(2),(1-i)/sqrt(2)),

which commutes with c, one obtains exactly

  D a_0^2 D^*=M0_v117,
  D c D^*=U_v117=c.                                   (10)

The determinant of the displayed D is -1, but multiplying it by any scalar
whose cube is -1 leaves (10) unchanged and places the conjugator in SU(3).
Hence (10) is uniqueness in the correctly marked unitary category, not only
equality of spectra.
