1. Affine uniformization and the eight real forms
=================================================

With O=(0,i), T=(1+i)/2 and the square lattice, the formulas

  Qz=iz,
  hz=-z+T,
  kappaz=-i bar(z)+T

give the stated curve automorphisms. Exact affine composition produces

  rho_(a,b)=h^a Q^b kappa,  a in Z2, b in Z4,

and every rho_(a,b) squares to a lattice translation. Thus all eight maps are
anti-holomorphic involutions of E.

The fixed-locus substitution

  x=e^(i b pi/4)t,  y=i^a v

gives

  v^2=(-1)^a[(-1)^b t^4-1].

Together with the action on the two infinity points, this gives component
counts

  (1,0,1,0; 1,2,1,2).

Hence precisely hQkappa and hQ^3kappa have two ovals. For a real elliptic
curve these are precisely the dividing forms. Their halves have width 1/2
and circumference 1, so the modulus is 1/2 and the Schottky double has tau=i.
Q rotates the two cut charts. It is not the half translation along one
boundary circle.

On the base x coordinate the two dividing forms act as x -> +/-i bar(x).
Their mark permutations agree exactly with the midpoint family in the cited
v519 code. The v519 result remains a free finite-carrier reflection test; it
does not identify its state with the annulus Calderon state.

2. Schottky reflection, elliptic translation and annulus glide
================================================================

The holomorphic involution

  delta_E=hQ^2,  delta_E z=z+T

is fixed-point free and has order two on E. It commutes on E with both
dividing real forms. On the chosen annulus half it descends to

  delta_A(t,s)=(1/2-t,s+1/2).

This map exchanges the boundary components and is free. Its square shifts s
by one, hence is the identity on the annulus base. The quotient is a Moebius
band.

The following three operations are distinct:

  rho is the fixed-boundary Schottky and OS reflection;
  delta_E is the free holomorphic two-torsion translation on E;
  delta_A is the orientation-reversing annulus deck represented upstairs by
  the glide g=delta_E rho.

The tangential half translation in delta_A is geometrically real. It cannot
be deleted when computing spin or Pin squares.

3. The fixed NS pullback and the central sign
================================================

The selected nontrivial flat spin line has character

  chi(A)=chi(B)=-1,  chi(A+B)=+1.

It is the unique nontrivial Q-invariant spin character. It has no constant
zero mode and restricts to NS spin structure on both boundary components.

Therefore

  U_rho U_delta=chi(A) U_delta U_rho
                     =-U_delta U_rho.

Also 2T=A+B, so

  U_delta^2=chi(A+B)=+I.

For g(z)=-bar(z)+T one has g^2(z)=z+B, hence

  U_g^2=chi(B)=-I.

The spin character is also selected internally by these requirements. Q

Q-invariant nontrivial spin line. This concerns the fixed raw pullback
realization. It does not authorize an added scalar phase.

4. Complex-linear Pin operator and anti-linear Real lift
=========================================================

Let

  D=sigma_t partial_t+sigma_s partial_s,
  sigma_t=[[0,1],[1,0]],
  sigma_s=[[0,-i],[i,0]].

The derivative of g changes the t sign and preserves the s sign. For the
complex-linear pullback U_g, coefficient comparison gives

  D sigma_s U_g=sigma_s U_g D.

Since sigma_s^2=I and U_g^2=-I,

  A=sigma_s U_g,  A^2=-I.

A is complex linear, so its minimal polynomial divides x^2+1 and it has the
ordinary c=+i and c=-i eigenspaces. On either eigenspace the quotient operator
c^-1 A squares to +I. This proves the claimed order-four Pin descent at the
level stated in the source.

The Real lift j_g is anti-linear and is a different operator. Because
bar(sigma_s)=-sigma_s and j_g^2=-I,

  (sigma_s j_g)^2=+I.

An anti-linear operator has no ordinary complex +/-i eigenspace
decomposition. The corrected source now states this explicitly, so the
earlier type ambiguity is resolved.

8. Review of the boundary continuation
=======================================

The coordinate change theta=2pi s is unitary from L2(ds) to
L2(dtheta/(2pi)) and carries H1_NS to H1_NS. On that domain,

  R B_Sigma R^-1=2pi B_cov.

have eigenvalues +i and -i. Writing the two mode classes with the periodic
base coordinate x=2theta gives exactly

  B_cov=2(B_1/4 direct_sum B_3/4).

Multiplication by exp(i theta) maps the 1/4 block to the 3/4 block and maps
the 3/4 block to the 1/4 block with one base winding. Thus the factor two and
the carry matrix in the boundary report are exact operator statements, not a
spectral analogy.

For D_+=partial_t+B_Sigma on a strip of width W, the solution graph has

  T^2(1+T^2)^-1=(1+exp(2W B_Sigma))^-1.

At W=1/2 this becomes

  C_A=(1+exp(2pi B_cov))^-1.

The opposite face has I-C_A. Therefore the sign in the report is correct for
the stated second-face orientation. Reversing the face or boundary
orientation replaces C_A by its complement and reverses the winding factor.
This label must remain part of any later P1 identification.

The trace norm calculation is also correct:

  ||C_A-P_-||_1
    =2 sum_(k>=0) 1/(1+exp(2pi(k+1/2)))

The squared winding gives (1-z)^2. This agrees with the previously proved
pair-index theorem and with the fold carry. The result is an ordinary
