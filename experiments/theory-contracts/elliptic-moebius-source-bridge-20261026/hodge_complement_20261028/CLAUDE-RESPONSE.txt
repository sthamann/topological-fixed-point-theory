I verified the algebra you supplied and traced the chain to the first arrow that is not proved. Short version: the exact identities you state are correct, the "inclusive response" is genuinely source-backed and is *not* a clock/instrument — but it is the **Spin(10) Dirac/Clebsch channel**, which provably is **not** the Petersen channel. The Petersen operator is a different invariant (the SU(5)-ε contraction, your Chain 1), and it belongs to a different existing E8 grading. That reassignment is the one strong continuation.

### 1. What checks out exactly

Hence $C_m^2=P(\text{no }m)$ (rank-6), and since each 2-word omits exactly $5-2=3$ indices, $\sum_m C_m^\dagger C_m=\sum_m P(\text{no }m)=3I$. So $\{C_m/\sqrt3\}$ is a legitimate Kraus set, $\Phi=\tfrac13\sum_m C_m\rho C_m$ is CPTP, and on diagonal input the three outputs $\mathrm{comp}(m,i,j)$ are exactly the three 2-words **disjoint** from $\{ij\}$, i.e. the Petersen neighbours. Thus $\Phi|_{\text{pop}}=A_{\mathrm{Pet}}/3$, with no $t$, filter, or reset. This is correct and is categorically different from the failed single-Kraus $C/\sqrt3$ (operator norm $\sqrt{5/3}$). The SU(5) reading is also right: $\ast(e_m\wedge\cdot)$ is built from $\varepsilon$, so the family is the invariant intertwiner $\mathbf{10}\to\overline{\mathbf{10}}\otimes\overline{\mathbf 5}$; the charge ledger $X_e+X_f+X_m=0$ is the statement that $\varepsilon_{ijklm}\neq0$ needs a full partition, so output lands in the conjugate. All consistent.

**Chain 3 (E8).** The grading is real: it is the $\mathrm{Spin}(16)\supset\mathrm{Spin}(10)\times\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ decomposition inside $E_8=\mathfrak{so}(16)\oplus\mathbf{128}$, with the $\mathbb Z_4$ supplied by the $SU(4)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ **center**: $g_0=(45,1)+(1,15)$, $g_2=(10,6)$ from the adjoint $\mathbf{120}$; $g_1=(16,4)$, $g_3=(\overline{16},\overline 4)$ from $\mathbf{128}$. **Normalization.** On the 16, $\sum_{i=1}^{10}\gamma_i^\dagger\gamma_i=10\,I_{16}$; on the 4, $\sum_{a=1}^{6}\gamma_a^\dagger\gamma_a=6\,I_4$. Hence
$$\sum_{ia}B_{ia}^\dagger B_{ia}=\tfrac14(10\,I_{16})\otimes(6\,I_4)=15\,I_{64}.$$
So $\mathcal E(\rho)=\tfrac1{15}\sum_{ia}B_{ia}\rho B_{ia}^\dagger$ is CPTP and factors as $\big[\tfrac1{10}\sum_i\gamma_i\cdot\gamma_i^\dagger\big]\otimes\big[\tfrac16\sum_a\gamma_a\cdot\gamma_a^\dagger\big]$. **Chain 3 → Chain 2.** With $\gamma_{2k-1}=a_k+a_k^\dagger$, $\gamma_{2k}=-i(a_k-a_k^\dagger)$, the cross terms cancel and
$$\tfrac1{10}\sum_{i=1}^{10}\gamma_i\rho\gamma_i=\tfrac15\sum_{k=1}^5\big(a_k\rho a_k^\dagger+a_k^\dagger\rho a_k\big),$$
which is exactly Chain 2's $\sum K\rho K^\dagger$ with $K_i=a_i/\sqrt5$, $K_{i+5}=a_i^\dagger/\sqrt5$, and $\sum K^\dagger K=\tfrac15\sum_k(a_k^\dagger a_k+a_ka_k^\dagger)=I$ (CAR). **This identity is correct.** Good: the E8 g2 inclusive response reduces precisely to the fermionic Dirac channel.

### 2. The first unproved — and false — arrow

The implicit next arrow, "this a/a† channel gives the Petersen step $A_{\mathrm{Pet}}/3$ on the ten 2-words," does not hold. The Dirac channel **flips fermion parity**: on $\Lambda^2$ (even) one step produces $\Lambda^3\oplus\Lambda^1$ (odd). It is not an endomorphism of the ten, so it cannot be $A_{\mathrm{Pet}}/3$ in one step; its full-Fock population graph is $Q_5$, whose folded form is Clebsch, with Petersen only as a **distance-2 subconstituent** around a chosen base vertex.

What does the channel give when you return to $\Lambda^2$ (two steps, back to even parity)? Compute the 2-word outcomes of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^2$ on $|ij\rangle$:

- $ij\xrightarrow{a_k^\dagger} ijk\xrightarrow{a_\ell}$: remove $k\Rightarrow ij$ (stay); remove $i$ or $j\Rightarrow jk,ik$ — **share one index**.
- $ij\xrightarrow{a_j} i\xrightarrow{a_\ell^\dagger} i\ell$: $\ell=j\Rightarrow ij$ (stay); else $i\ell$ — **share one index**.

Every 2-word return either stays or **shares exactly one index**. None are disjoint. So the $\Lambda^2\to\Lambda^2$ block of the squared Dirac channel is supported on $\{\text{diagonal}\}\cup T(5)$, where $T(5)=J(5,2)$ is the triangular graph — the **complement of Petersen**. 

This is decisive: the existing D5+A3 inclusive response, whether read one step (parity-flip, Clebsch) or two steps (even return), **never** yields $A_{\mathrm{Pet}}$; its even return is the Petersen *complement*. The disjointness/Petersen relation is produced only by the $\varepsilon$-contraction $C_m$ (Chain 1), which maps $\Lambda^2\to\Lambda^2$ (parity-preserving) via the complement, not by the Spin(10) Clifford sum. So your sentence "intrinsically it should be an SU5 intertwiner … output is conjugate representation" is right *about Chain 1*, but it is a **different** invariant from the Chain 3 Clifford $\gamma_i$. The chain silently crosses from the $\varepsilon$-intertwiner to the $\gamma$-intertwiner; those are orthogonal couplings ($\mathbf{10}\cdot\mathbf{10}\cdot\overline{\mathbf 5}$ via $\varepsilon$ versus $\mathbf{16}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{16}}\cdot\mathbf{10}$ via Clifford). **That crossing is the first unproved arrow, and as a $\Lambda^2$-endomorphism it is false.**
