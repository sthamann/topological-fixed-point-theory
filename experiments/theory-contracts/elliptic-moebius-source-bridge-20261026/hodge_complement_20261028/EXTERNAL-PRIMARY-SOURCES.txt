PRIMARY LITERATURE USED TO IDENTIFY THE FERMIONIC OPERATION
Read 28 October 2026

Grigori G. Amosov and Sergey N. Filippov,
Spectral properties of reduced fermionic density operators and parity
superselection rule, Quantum Information Processing16,2 (2017).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01828
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11128-016-1467-9

The authors define the particle-reduction operation as
Phi(rho)=sum_i a_i rho a_i^dagger. On a fixed n-particle sector its trace
is n, so division by n gives the particle-loss channel. This supports
the established mathematical reading of the exterior channel used here.
The identification with the specific Petersen response and the actual
TFPT E8 source is computed separately in our exact source checks.

Marius Lemm and Mark M. Wilde,
Information-theoretic limitations on approximate quantum cloning and
broadcasting, Physical Review A96,012304 (2017).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07569
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.012304

The paper treats antisymmetric particle subspaces, particle-loss partial
traces and their relation to recovery/cloning maps. It supplies context
for the known fermionic operation. It is not evidence that P1 selects
the event record, source insertion, physical dynamics or initial state.

These are primary research sources. Their use does not make the general
fermionic channel a newly discovered mathematical operation.
