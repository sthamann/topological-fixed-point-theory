Exact type theorem

   [J(f),J(g)] = i sigma(f,g) I,
   W(f)W(g) = exp(-i sigma(f,g)/2) W(f+g).
Changing the overall convention for sigma changes none of the conclusions.

Let Theta(W(f))=W(Lf), with real linear L. Let c=+1 for scalar linearity and
c=-1 for conjugate linearity. Let o=+1 for preservation of product order and
o=-1 for its reversal. Comparing the two sides of the Weyl product gives
   sigma(Lf,Lg) = c o sigma(f,g).                         (1)
This is an equality of forms, not a congruence modulo a phase: all real
scalings of f and g must work.

symplectic. For a chiral spatial reflection r f(x)=f(-x),
   sigma(rf,rg) = -sigma(f,g).
