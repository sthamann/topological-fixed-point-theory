3. Any rational lift would have to be a tiny polynomial
========================================================

Let G be a rational solution of (1).

At an ordinary point, a pole of order m>=1 is impossible: G' would have a
term of order m+1 while both coefficient matrices in (1) are regular there.

At a puncture z=i^k, the residue operator acting on a leading matrix C is

  L_k(C)=A_k C-C A_(-k).                               (7)

Since the two residues in (7) are conjugate Hermitian matrices with spectrum
(5), the spectrum of L_k consists of their pairwise differences

  {0,+/-1/3,+/-2/3}.

A pole C t^(-m) would require L_k(C)=-m C. No negative integer occurs, so G
has no pole at any of the four punctures.

Near z=0 the transformed infinity term is

  z^(-2)A(1/z)=B0/z+O(1).                              (8)

The same leading-order argument excludes a pole because B0 has only the
positive eigenvalues 2,1,1. It also gives G(0)=0. If

  G=sum_(n>=0) G_n z^n,

then the indicial equation is

  n G_n=G_n B0                                       (9)

at the first nonzero order. Consequently the first column begins at order
z^2, while the second and third columns begin at order z.

Finally, at infinity the leading part of (1) is

  G'=(B0/z)G+O(z^(-2)G).                              (10)

For a polynomial leading term z^d C, equation (10) says dC=B0 C. Hence the
first row has degree at most two and the second and third rows degree at most
one. Every rational solution of (1) must therefore have exactly the bounded
form

         [ a z^2   b z+c z^2   d z+e z^2 ]
  G(z)= [ 0       f z           g z       ].           (11)
         [ 0       h z           j z       ]

This is not a guessed truncation. Regularity, (5), the 0-infinity exchange
and the Birkhoff-Grothendieck exponents (2,1,1) force (11).
