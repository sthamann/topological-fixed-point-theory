1. The primary construction fixes the clutch

The standard invertible-double theorem is more specific than an arbitrary
double.  On a product collar it writes

  D=J(partial_x+B),
  J^2=-I,  J^*=-J,  B^*=B,  JB=-BJ,

and glues D to -D by the boundary clutch J=c(n_inward).  Booß-Bavnbek and
Lesch, Proposition 1.4, use exactly the map

  f(x) -> J f(-x)

to identify the collar expressions of D and -D.  Green's formula and weak
unique continuation then prove that the resulting closed operator is
invertible.  Thus the clutch is part of the canonical double; it is not a
freely selected spin closure.

The AP conclusion below uses the exact product hypotheses.  A
normal-dependent coefficient or a different boundary clutch is outside this
calculation.

2. Independent sign calculation on the interval

Use the actual constant product operator on 0<=x<=ell,

  D_1=N(partial_x+B),
  N^2=-I,  N^*=-N,  NB=-BN.                         (1)

The inward normals give

  J_0=N,                 J_ell=-N.                    (2)

Write the reversed second copy with r=2 ell-x.  The operator -D on that copy
is

  D_2=N(partial_r-B).                                  (3)

The canonical clutch conditions are therefore

  v(0)=N u(0),              v(ell)=-N u(ell).          (4)

Put the second copy in the same fixed frame as the first by w=Nv.  Both the
input and output frames must be transformed.  Direct multiplication gives

  N D_2 N^-1=N(partial_r+B).                           (5)

At the joined point r=ell,

  w(ell)=N[-N u(ell)]=u(ell),

whereas after the full normal circuit,

  w(2 ell)=N[N u(0)]=-u(0).                            (6)

Consequently the canonical product double is the single operator

  D_tilde=N(partial_r+B)

on a circle of length 2 ell with the antiperiodic domain

  H1_AP={f in H1([0,2 ell]): f(2 ell)=-f(0)}.          (7)

This is a sign consequence of the two inward Clifford multiplications.  It
does not follow merely from the numerical boundary covariance G.

3. The actual TFPT coefficient satisfies the hypotheses

The existing graded-boundary construction fixes

  N=[[0,-1],[1,0]],     Z=diag(1,-1),     S=NZ,
  B_Phi=Z tensor A-S tensor Phi,
  Gamma_tot=Z tensor gamma_F.                           (8)

Here A is the even tangential connection, Phi is the odd bounded finite
coefficient, and the family is normal-independent.  The exact matrix
relations are

  NZ=-ZN,  NS=-SN,

so

  N B_Phi+B_Phi N=0.                                   (9)

This is also checked on the actual full96 v252 coefficient in
graded_boundary_functor/check_finite_typing.py.  On the positive total-grade
chart E,

  B_Phi E=E h_Phi,
  h_Phi=A gamma_F-i gamma_F Phi,

and the selfdual charged chart is

  B_Phi = h_Phi directsum(-bar(h_Phi)).                (10)

The doubling and AP condition act on the entire normal times full96 space.
They are not a one-charge-leg boundary choice.  In particular, N
anticommutes with Gamma_tot, so each clutch crossing exchanges the two total
grades.  The two crossings give N^2=-I and return the grade with the minus
holonomy in (6).  Formula (10) may be used to evaluate the two charge legs,
but neither leg may be silently discarded.  Since cosh is even,

  X(-bar(h_Phi))=bar(X(h_Phi)),

with the actual multiplicities retained in any determinant or trace.

4. The deck lift is not the old reflection involution

Before the fixed-frame unfolding, the natural exchange on the pair domain
v=Ju is

  T(u,v)=(-v,u).                                        (11)

It preserves the clutch domain and satisfies

  T^2=-I,
  T (D directsum -D)=-(D directsum -D) T.              (12)

Thus this canonical spin deck lift has order four on fields.  Multiplying it
by the old scalar complex unit i0 would make its square +I, but that operation
changes its real and KO typing and is not supplied by the invertible-double
theorem.  In particular, (11) is not automatically the original geometric
tau with tau^2=1, the boundary polarization iC, or a physical OS reflection.
Any such identification must compare the phase, grading and anti/linearity
in the original chart.  The AP normal domain remains valid without making
that additional identification.

There are already two distinct involutive lifts on the unfolded AP circle.
Let

  (P_r f)(r)=f(2 ell-r).

Using the old scalar complex unit i0 and the actual total grading gives

  R_odd =i0 N P_r,
  R_even=Gamma_tot N P_r.                              (13)

The exact operator algebra is

  R_odd^2=I,       R_odd D_tilde R_odd=-D_tilde,
  R_even^2=I,      R_even D_tilde R_even=+D_tilde.     (14)

Here P_r reverses partial_r, N anticommutes with B_Phi and Gamma_tot, and
Gamma_tot anticommutes with D_tilde.  In the two-copy description the
grading compatible with the J clutch is diag(Gamma_tot,-Gamma_tot).  Equations
(13)-(14) show that even after an involutive phase choice there are two
different operator types.  The canonical-double theorem does not select one
as the physical KO, deck or OS lift.

5. What the one canonical double now derives

For the constant product coefficient, (1) and (9) give

  D_tilde^2=-partial_r^2+B_Phi^2.                      (15)

Hence the canonical double supplies the AP second-order spectrum.  With
E_mu=sqrt(B_Phi^2+mu^2), a finite tangential Fourier cutoff gives, up to an
E_mu-independent normalization,

  log det_AP,cut(-partial_r^2+B_Phi^2+mu^2)
    =Tr_cut[2 log(2 cosh(ell E_mu))].                  (16)

On the full tangential circle this splits formally as

  2 ell Tr(E_mu)+2 Tr log(I+exp(-2 ell E_mu)).          (17)

The first term diverges and needs the already specified zeta or common local
renormalization.  Equality of absolute determinants cannot be inferred by
interchanging the two spectral sums without that prescription; local
multiplicative-anomaly terms may remain.  The second term is canonical for
the product operator: exp(-2 ell E_mu) is trace class and defines the
positive gluing factor

  det_F(I+exp(-2 ell E_mu))^2.                          (18)

Thus the canonical double fixes the AP spectrum and the Fredholm gluing
factor.  It does not choose the bulk counterterm, a first-order determinant
phase or a selfdual Pfaffian convention.  All traces still retain both charge
legs and the actual finite multiplicities.

Multiplication by -N identifies the unregulated first-order complex operator

  -N D_tilde=partial_r+B_Phi.                           (19)

Its AP Green branch between opposite points of the normal circle is

  S_AP(ell,0)
    =exp(-ell B_Phi)(I+exp(-2 ell B_Phi))^-1
    =(2 cosh(ell B_Phi))^-1.                           (20)

Under the charged chart this is X(h_Phi) on one leg and its conjugate on the
other.  Therefore, once the original source operator is indeed this
canonical product double, the AP normal spectrum, the positive Fredholm
gluing factor (18), and the exact first-order midpoint Green operator (20)
follow from the same given D.  No separate AP boundary selection or fitted
cross kernel is needed.  The absolute full determinant still requires the
common bulk regularization just described.
