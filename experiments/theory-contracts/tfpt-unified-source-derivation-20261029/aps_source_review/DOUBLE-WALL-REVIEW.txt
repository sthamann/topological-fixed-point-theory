2. What D1 proves, and its first additional input

Let h be the actual given selfadjoint charged boundary generator. Impose
the homogeneous closed operator L=partial_r+h with antiperiodic condition
on a circle of length beta=2ell. In each spectral component of h its Green
kernel for -beta<tau=r-s<beta, tau!=0, is

  G(tau)=exp(-tau h)[(1-n)1_(tau>0)-n1_(tau<0)],
  n=(1+exp(beta h))^-1.

The jump is I and G(tau+beta)=-G(tau). Functional calculus therefore gives

  G(ell)=exp(-ell h)/(1+exp(-2ell h))
         =(2 cosh(ell h))^-1.

These are the familiar occupation and crossing blocks of the already
constructed homogeneous graph. They supply a correct closed-source
realization under the given assumptions. They do not remove the additional
assertion, explicitly introduced by Claude, that the actual original
Schwinger functions equal these particular closed Green functions.

Local face orientation/reality and global spin holonomy are distinct data.
For example, any linear clutch U satisfying the local compatibility
condition C1 U=U C0 has -U satisfying the SAME condition. This homogeneous
compatibility equation cannot select the gluing sign. A theorem with a
specified global spin bundle, lift and clutch can select a closure, but
those data and their induced domain must be tracked. They are not encoded
in the displayed formula C_faces alone.

An ordinary fermionic trace has antiperiodic coherent-state closure; the
corresponding supertrace has periodic closure. Using that familiar rule
presupposes the relevant trace/source realization. It is not a derivation
of that realization from local face orientation. In particular an arbitrary
periodic spin structure on a geometric double is not ruled out merely by
naming the supertrace of a different chosen quantization.

3. The smallest exact finite-wall test

Use precisely the stated normal matrices

  N=[[0,-1],[1,0]], Z=diag(1,-1), S=NZ=[[0,1],[1,0]].

Thus N^2=-I, Z^2=S^2=I, NZ=S, ZN=-S, and S anticommutes with N and Z.
At one actual tangential Fourier value p, take finite-dimensional constant
Phi=Phi*, a smooth real periodic scalar sigma(r), and the scalar
antiperiodic domain on the circle of length 2ell. Set

  Q=N partial_r+Z sigma(r)Phi,
  D=Q+pS.

Internal tensor identities are implicit; Phi commutes with N,Z,S. Q and D
are selfadjoint on H1_antiperiodic by bounded perturbation of N partial_r.
They have compact resolvent. On H2_antiperiodic, with the corresponding
first-derivative boundary trace, direct multiplication gives

  {S,Q}=0,
  Q^2=-partial_r^2+sigma(r)^2 Phi^2+S sigma'(r)Phi,
  D^2=Q^2+p^2 I.                                      (1)

In particular the derivative term has the PLUS sign. To test an exact
lambda_D=+/-p it suffices to solve Q psi=0. Multiplying this equation by N
gives

  partial_r psi=sigma(r)(S Phi)psi,
  psi(r)=exp(S Phi F(r))psi(0),
  F(r)=integral_0^r sigma(u)du.                         (2)

For the claimed odd periodic wall profile, including a profile of the
displayed tanh(sin) type, the integral around the circle is zero. Its normal
transfer in (2) is consequently I. The antiperiodic boundary condition
requires that transfer to act as -I on the initial value. Hence psi(0)=0,
and there is NO nonzero normal zero mode.

In fact the conclusion is stronger within this restricted commuting class:
for any real sigma and constant Hermitian Phi, exp(S Phi integral sigma)
is positive definite, so it has no eigenvalue -1. No scalar antiperiodic
normal zero mode exists even without the zero-mean premise. This statement
does not cover noncommuting background transport or a different clutch.

Compact resolvent now gives mu0=min |spec Q|>0 for each fixed such source.
By (1), every spectral value of D satisfies

  |lambda_D| >= sqrt(p^2+mu0^2) > |p|.                 (3)

The anticommutation pairs Q-eigenvalues mu and -mu, and the corresponding
two-dimensional block has D-eigenvalues +/-sqrt(p^2+mu^2). Thus exact
eigenvalues +/-p are absent. mu0 can become very small in a separation or
large-mass limit; (3) is not a uniform lower bound over such limits.

The statement that the modes are exact at |p| and the later allowance for
exponentially small coupling are different statements. Only the latter can
describe approximate isolated-wall modes on this finite circle. The number
0.2501 reported for p=0.25 already fails equality at the printed precision;
the analytic argument, rather than that rounding, decides the exact claim.

There is also an explicit inverse in this SAME zero-mean, constant-Phi,
scalar-antiperiodic class. Write L=2ell, K=S Phi and
Fmat(r)=exp(K integral_0^r sigma), so Fmat(L)=I. Since
Q=N(partial_r-K sigma), variation of constants and the anti-I closure give

  Q^-1(r,s)=Fmat(r)[1_(r>s)-1/2]Fmat(s)^-1(-N).         (4)

The derivative of the bracket is delta(r-s); the endpoint values are
opposites. Thus (4) maps L2 to the actual H1 antiperiodic domain and is the
unique inverse, not just an exclusion of one trial zero mode. It requires
exactly the fixed commuting coefficient and zero-mean premises displayed
here; it is not an arbitrary normally varying background Green formula.

4. What the infinite single-wall calculation does prove

Retain constant Hermitian Phi but put r on R. Suppose sigma approaches -1
at -infinity and +1 at +infinity. Then F(r) in (2) grows as |r| at both ends.
For a Phi-eigenvector of eigenvalue m!=0 and an S-eigenvector of eigenvalue
s=+/-1, a normal solution is square integrable exactly when s m<0. Thus

  s=-sign(m),
  D|_(ker Q)=p S,
  dim ker(Q)|_(S=+1)-dim ker(Q)|_(S=-1)
       =n_-(Phi)-n_+(Phi)=-signature(Phi).              (5)

The opposite kink reverses the sign. A zero eigenvalue of Phi gives a
constant normal solution, not a localized L2 mode on this infinite line.

For finite Hermitian Phi satisfying {gamma_F,Phi}=0, gamma_F pairs each
nonzero eigenvalue m with -m. Therefore signature(Phi)=0. Equation (5) then
has zero net index in THIS infinite, one-dimensional, real scalar wall
ansatz. The finite double has no exact zero mode by section3; assigning
exact chiralities separately to its two coupled walls is not justified.

5. The local deck relation is not a smooth global wall prescription

The earlier exact deck calculation with S R_r does change the displayed
Yukawa coefficient to -Phi on the reflected coordinate chart, under its
stated common-domain and coefficient-transport assumptions. A physical
global mass field additionally requires the actual spinor trivialization,
clutch and internal lift. A sign in two local descriptions alone does not
prove a gauge-independent domain wall in a globally identified bundle.

If one DOES choose a single trivialization and an ordinary scalar odd
periodic sigma, smoothness requires sigma to vanish at reflection-fixed
seams. Extending a nonzero constant mass with the opposite sign produces a
jump unless the coefficient is changed near the seams. The tanh(sin)
profile supplies precisely such a new smooth interpolation. It is not
derived by the local matrix S alone.

For a specified smooth profile and a fixed closed invertible domain the
resolvent identity delta(D^-1)=-D^-1(delta D)D^-1 is valid. It does not imply
that finite-wall eigenvalues stay exactly at |p|. Their normal splittings
in (3) can respond to the mass profile. Nor can the homogeneous KMS formula
of section2 be applied to an arbitrary normally varying coefficient: the
existing normal_dependent_transport result carries its actual ordered
transfer and full crossing blocks without making that substitution.

6. Preserve the older positive normal-index result and the target scope

The existing spin normal geometry is not classified by (5). On the stated
Spin S2=CP1, K^(1/2)=O(-1), and the actual twist L2=O(1) gives

  ker D_(N,L2)^+ =H0(O(-1) tensor O(1))=H0(O)=C,
  ker D_(N,L2)^- =H1(O)=0.

This operator has index one. That topological index is not the signature
of a finite Yukawa matrix. The old result remains a positive conditional
TFPT normal attachment under its explicit operator and family premises.

Consequently the proposed inference that unit winding generally requires
a gamma_F-even mass of nonzero signature does not follow from the real
one-dimensional wall calculation. Neither the finite-wall correction nor
the odd-Phi signature identity is a no-go theorem for full P1, physical
chirality, or other already present normal geometries.

The useful retained result is precise: a given homogeneous antiperiodic
closed source reproduces the known graph blocks, and a chosen normally
varying source is already carried by the full fixed Clifford chart. The
new asserted origin and exact finite-wall modes are not established by
those facts. The finite normal-zero calculation above directly disproves
the latter assertion in its stated antiperiodic script class.
