5. TWO MAJORANA GENERATORS DO NOT DETERMINE RELATIVE PARITY

The smallest existing charged CAR control is one mode a,a*,
{a,a*}=1. Its two real Majorana operators are

 gamma1=a+a*, gamma2=i(a*-a).

Nevertheless |0> and |1>=a*|0> have opposite relative fermion parity.
Their selfdual basis projections are complementary rank1 projections
on C+bar(C), so the relevant relative intersection has complex dimension1.
Explicitly, P=diag(1,0), Q=diag(0,1), and C0 is the antilinear flip.
Then C0 P C0=1-P, C0 Q C0=1-Q, and
dim_C(Ran P intersect Ran(1-Q))=1.
Thus the statement 'one complex mode is two Majoranas, therefore its
Araki relative parity is even' is false already in this two-state example.

The same example also shows why a nonzero odd transition amplitude is
not a state: phi(A)=<1|A|0> is nonzero, but phi(1)=0. Any positive
functional with phi(1)=0 is identically zero. Hence this phi cannot be
a positive normalized CAR state. With three modes the functional
<111|A|000> has precisely the analogous odd three-insertion amplitude.

Both diagonal vector states, <0|A|0> and <1|A|1>, ARE positive,
normalized and invariant under the CAR grading. In particular an odd
relative vacuum parity does not prevent an even positive CAR state.
The occupied vacuum merely lies in the odd Fock-parity sector relative
to the chosen empty vacuum. Zero overlap is not a proof of an anomalous
or undefined state and does not force an additional odd partner sector.
