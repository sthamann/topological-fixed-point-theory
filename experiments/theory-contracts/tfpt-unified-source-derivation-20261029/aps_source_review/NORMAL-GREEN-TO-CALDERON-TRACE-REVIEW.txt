30 October 2026

Question

Claude39's bare notation S2=G is ill typed: a bulk Green kernel and a
two-boundary Calderon projector are not the same operator.  Does the original
constant product collar nevertheless contain an exact, choice-free map from
the Green kernel to that projector?  Is the antiperiodic normal double used in
this map fixed by the original source, or only one compatible closure?

Verdict

The ORIGINAL homogeneous collar already fixes the closed Poisson range

  graph(exp(-ell h)).

Together with its positive boundary metric, this range fixes the orthogonal
Calderon projector, independently of any chosen compatible closed Green
realization.  No new antiperiodic selection gate is needed for that existing
G contract.  For a selfadjoint tangential operator h the result is

  G = [[A,X],[X,1-A]],
  A=(1+exp(-2 ell h))^-1,
  X=exp(-ell h)(1+exp(-2 ell h))^-1.

The antiperiodic circle of length 2 ell supplies one stronger realization:
the boundary trace of its raw Green Poisson formula is already this orthogonal
G.  This genuine Green-to-Calderon trace identity uses the jump, the sign of
the negative Green branch and both boundary limits.  It is not the naked
equality of a bulk two-point function with a boundary projector.

The antiperiodic closure is actual data in the already constructed elliptic
NS source and in the period-two NS cover.  There the raw Green trace identity
is an original source identity inside the explicitly retained free charged
Gaussian premise.  It is not forced for every primitive P1 Green realization
by a local Spin(2) lift with square -I alone.  The transport from the abstract
P1 datum to this particular elliptic NS source is needed only if one wants to
identify their BULK Schwinger kernels or quantizations.  It is not a
prerequisite for the original orthogonal Calderon projector obtained from the
Poisson range and boundary metric.  This review does not identify h with the
old longitudinal quarter generator, and it supplies no new state, physical
time or P1 quantization rule.

1. Exact scalar identity

Fix ell>0 and one spectral value lambda of h.  Put

  q=exp(-ell lambda),
  a=1/(1+q^2),
  x=q/(1+q^2),
  c=q^2/(1+q^2)=1-a.

On the antiperiodic circle of length 2 ell the inverse of partial_s+lambda has
the two branches

  S(tau)= exp(-tau lambda)a,                    0<tau<2 ell,
  S(tau)=-exp(-(tau+2 ell)lambda)a,          -2 ell<tau<0.

They satisfy

  S(0+)-S(0-)=a+q^2 a=1,
  S(tau+2 ell)=-S(tau).

For 0<s<ell, the Poisson field produced from an arbitrary boundary source
pair is

  psi(s)=S(s)f_0-S(s-ell)f_ell.                         (1)

The interior limits of (1) are

  psi(0+)   =a f_0+x f_ell,
  psi(ell-) =x f_0+c f_ell.

Thus the normal trace of the Green Poisson operator is

  g_lambda=[[a,x],[x,c]].                               (2)

Directly,

  a^2+x^2=a,
  x^2+c^2=c,
  ax+xc=x.

Therefore g_lambda*=g_lambda and g_lambda^2=g_lambda.  Its range is the
one-dimensional graph {(u,q u)}.  This proves both the signs and the
normalization without equating the Green kernel itself to g_lambda.

2. Full selfadjoint operator

Let h be an arbitrary selfadjoint operator.  The transfer

  T=exp(-ell h)

can be unbounded when h is unbounded below, but it is a closed positive
operator.  The scalar functions

  a(lambda)=1/(1+exp(-2 ell lambda)),
  x(lambda)=exp(-ell lambda)/(1+exp(-2 ell lambda)),
  c(lambda)=1-a(lambda)

are bounded.  Borel functional calculus therefore defines bounded operators
A, X and C=1-A on the whole boundary Hilbert space.  The scalar identities of
section 1 hold almost everywhere for the spectral measure of h, hence

  G=[[A,X],[X,C]]=G*=G^2.                              (3)

The standard closed-graph formula gives the same result:

  P_graph(T)=
    [[(1+T*T)^-1,          (1+T*T)^-1 T*],
     [T(1+T*T)^-1, T(1+T*T)^-1 T*]].                  (4)

Since T=T*, all four entries of (4) are A,X,X,C.  Thus (3) is the
orthogonal Calderon projector for the homogeneous collar, including the full
unbounded spectral case.  Boundary limits are understood in the usual trace
domain of the Poisson solution; the resulting projector is bounded.

3. Why antiperiodicity matters, and what does not depend on it

For comparison, suppose a periodic circle of length 2 ell is chosen and
1-exp(-2 ell h) has a bounded inverse.  Put

  A_p=(1-exp(-2 ell h))^-1,
  X_p=exp(-ell h)A_p.

The periodic Green branches have no minus sign across the closure.  Applying
the same two-boundary trace to (1) gives

  C_p=[[A_p,-X_p],[X_p,-exp(-2 ell h)A_p]].             (5)

Scalar functional calculus gives C_p^2=C_p, and its range is again graph(T),
but generally C_p is not selfadjoint.  Its orthogonal range projection is the
operator G in (3).  A zero mode of h makes the periodic inverse singular,
whereas the antiperiodic inverse and (3) remain regular with A=C=X=1/2 on that
mode.

Consequently the graph subspace and its orthogonal projector are intrinsic to
the given homogeneous collar transfer and its positive boundary metric.  The
statement that the RAW CLOSED Green jump matrix produces this orthogonal
projector directly does use the antiperiodic spin closure.  Local face reality
and the square of a local lift do not on their own choose that closure, but no
such choice is needed to orthogonally project onto the already fixed Poisson
range.

5. The original W_rel contract does not select J* M_mu

The original definition is

  Z_rel[J,eta,bar eta]=Z_adm[J,eta,bar eta]/Z_ref[0,0,0],
  W_rel=log Z_rel.

Its Schwinger distributions are defined by differentiating W_rel with respect
to the generic local source tuple (J,eta,bar eta).  These statements fix the
normalization and say how an ALREADY SPECIFIED local source insertion is read.
They do not provide an operator map from the symbol J or the Grassmann sources
to the later geometric Hom insertion M_mu.  In particular, the displayed
definition contains neither the q/p transport J_geom nor the contraction
M_mu, and it cannot by itself select

  K_mu=J_geom* M_mu.                                  (6)

The existing whole source supplies the two ingredients separately.  Its
transported CAR state and geometry give J_geom and the graph covariance G_q;
the original spin product plus its specified tangent or Clifford contraction
gives M_mu.  Once BOTH are supplied, (6) is uniquely typed on the q source and
the existing state fixes its response.  For graph test columns E_r,

  <E_r,G_q J_geom* M_mu E_s>
    =<J_geom E_r,M_mu E_s>.                           (7)

Thus the positive answer is precise: the given CAR source determines the
response of the specified insertion, including its endpoint, state and graph
factors.  W_rel alone neither chooses M_mu nor identifies its generic source
variable with (6).  This is the smallest decisive distinction between a
source functional and a source-to-operator dictionary.  It also agrees with
the recovered P1 kernel typing: the thin kernel does not specify its insertion
algebra, while the full operational seed may include such an algebra as
additional given data.
