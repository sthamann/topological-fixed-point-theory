C58: SHALE EQUIVALENCE, THOULESS PATCHES AND THE ACTUAL WEAK CAP
Independent targeted review, 30 October 2026.

1. What Hilbert-Schmidt equivalence actually proves
For two pure CAR polarizations P0 and Ph, the Shale-Stinespring/Araki
criterion

  P0-Ph is Hilbert-Schmidt                               (1)

puts their quasifree representations in the same Fock equivalence class. In a
fixed irreducible common representation the P0 quasifree vacuum ray therefore
exists and is unique up to phase. This is the precise positive part of C58.

Equation(1) does not by itself identify a single Gaussian boundary insertion
F_cap in the original positive-time OS algebra. The OS Hilbert space is a
completion of equivalence classes of positive-time admissible observables. A
vector in that completion need not be represented by one bounded, local or
polynomial observable in the original OS algebra. Nor is a Gaussian
implementer automatically localized on the declared boundary interval or
preserved by P_adm.

Reflection positivity says, once an admissible F has been supplied,

  ([F],T_ell[F])_OS>=0

It does not establish the positive-time membership of a proposed insertion.
An original identification still needs an explicit map from the common CAR
Fock vector to the QFT8 positive-time admissible algebra. This is the precise
gap in C58's Gaussian boundary-insertion claim; no operator-positivity claim is
attributed to C58 here.

2. Smallest counterexample to the universal Thouless claim
Take a two-dimensional one-particle space and

  Ph=|e1><e1|,  P0=|e2><e2|.

Then P0-Ph has finite rank and is Hilbert-Schmidt. Both projections have the
same rank and relative index zero. The P0 Slater vector is present in the Ph
Fock space, but it is orthogonal to the Ph vacuum. In particle-hole language it
is a complete finite particle-hole excitation.

A standard Thouless chart

  Omega0=c exp(pair creation by Z) Omega_h              (2)

has nonzero vacuum coefficient c and requires Ran P0 to be a graph over
Ran Ph. No finite graph operator Z represents the orthogonal example. It is a
boundary point of that chart, often written informally as Z=infinity. Therefore
Hilbert-Schmidt equivalence does not imply one universal Thouless exponential.

The correct alternatives are:

  * Big cell: P_h P0:Ran P0->Ran Ph is invertible, the vacuum overlap is
    nonzero, and a Hilbert-Schmidt graph kernel Z gives (2).
  * Zero-overlap but index zero: extract finitely many orthogonal
    particle-hole creators, then use a Thouless exponential on the transverse
    complement.
  * Nonzero relative index: additional unpaired finite creators or holes carry
    the relative charge; a pair-only exponential cannot represent the state.

The P0 vacuum ray is unique up to phase once the representation and zero-mode
occupation are fixed. The implementing unitary and an OS observable
representative are not unique: stabilizer factors, OS null vectors and dense
approximations remain.

3. Conditions for the actual weak family
For the already given constant finite coefficient and weak direction set

  h_p(t)=(pI+tQ)gamma_F-i gamma_F D_F,
  p in Z+alpha, alpha=1/4 or3/4,
  P0,p=1_(-infinity,0)(p gamma_F),
  Ph,p(t)=1_(-infinity,0)(h_p(t)).                      (3)

Here Q and D_F are fixed finite matrices. Write h_p(t)=p gamma_F+B(t), with
B(t) bounded independently of p. Since Q and D_F are constant along the
circle, h remains diagonal in the Fourier label p. What is lost for generic Q
is only the older reduction to independent anticommuting singular-value pairs,
not the mode-by-mode decomposition. For fixed finite t and sufficiently large
|p|, the gap of p gamma_F dominates B(t). Standard resolvent estimates give

  ||Ph,p(t)-P0,p||_HS=O(1/|p|).

Since the internal dimension is finite and sum_p |p|^-2 converges, the direct
sum difference is Hilbert-Schmidt. This reuses the previously recorded
one-dimensional whole-sea mechanism; it is not a new source theorem.

Only finitely many p can cross zero at fixed bounded t. Away from the critical
values satisfying

  0 in spec h_p(t),                                      (4)

Ph(t) is continuous, its relative Fredholm index is constant, and the old sea
is a well-defined ray in the common Fock representation. Starting in the
known no-crossing scalar-quarter component gives index zero until the first
crossing.

At a crossing, the coupled ground space is degenerate. The negative projection
alone does not fix the coupled vacuum ray or its relative charge. A spectral
section or the original modified-APS Lagrangian is needed only for that vacuum
and Thouless-chart description. The cap formula below is independent of the
occupation of ker h: both dGamma(h) and E0=Tr(h 1_(h<0)) are unchanged there.
Crossing may change the relative charge by a finite integer. This is a finite
event, not failure of Hilbert-Schmidt equivalence.

Even without a zero-energy crossing, a principal angle can reach pi/2 and make
the old/new vacuum overlap zero. The big-cell Thouless formula then fails while
the Fock vector and finite-time cap remain. The precise nonzero-overlap
condition is

  ker(Ph P0|Ran P0)=0

together with relative index zero. Under (1), absence of exact orthogonal
angles makes the corresponding Fredholm overlap product positive and supplies
the Hilbert-Schmidt graph patch.

4. The general cap survives every Thouless patch
For a finite set of modes, define Ph=1_(h<0) and the coupled ground energy

  E0=Tr(h Ph).

For the fixed old Slater vector Omega_P0, exterior functoriality gives

  Z_cap(P0,h;ell)
    =<Omega_P0,exp[-ell(dGamma(h)-E0)]Omega_P0>
    =exp(ell Tr(hPh))
       det_RanP0(P0 exp(-ell h) P0).                    (5)

Formula(5) remains valid when the ground-state overlap is zero. At finite
ell, exp(-ell h) is strictly positive and its compression to Ran P0 is
positive definite. A zero overlap only changes the large-ell leading sector.

For the whole circle, the ground-energy exponential and determinant must be
combined before the limit. They need not exist as two separate absolute
factors. The previously proved fixed-cap Fredholm limit supplies this
combination in its scalar-quarter scope. For the weak family, (1), the fixed
old P0 and coupled normal ordering give more than a merely formal candidate.
Let

  Z_p(ell)=<Omega_P0,p,exp(-ell H_exc,p)Omega_P0,p>.

Every finite-dimensional H_exc,p is nonnegative, hence 0<Z_p(ell)<=1 for
finite ell. For |p|>2||B(t)||, the projection estimate gives

  ||Ph,p-P0,p||_HS^2=O(p^-2).

More explicitly, put g=|p|, v=||B(t)|| and let r=48 be the rank of the
opposite unperturbed chirality in the actual 96-dimensional block. For p>0, a
negative normalized eigenvector u of h_p with eigenvalue lambda has wrong
chirality component

  ||P_plus u|| <= v/(g-lambda) <= v/(2g-v).

For p<0 the same estimate holds with the two chiralities exchanged.

Consequently

  Tr(Ph,p P_plus) <= r v^2/(2g-v)^2,                  (6a)

and the elementary product bound for the principal angles gives the same
upper bound for one minus the squared many-body ground overlap.

The squared ground overlap is therefore 1-O(p^-2). Resolving Omega_P0,p into
the coupled ground component and its excited components yields uniformly in
ell>=0

  0<=1-Z_p(ell)
    <=1-|<Omega_h,p,Omega_P0,p>|^2
    =O(p^-2).                                           (6)

Thus sum_p(1-Z_p)<infinity and the product of the positive Z_p converges to a
strictly positive whole-circle cap for every finite ell>0. Any zero overlap or
relative-charge change occurs in only finitely many low modes and contributes
a finite positive factor at finite ell. A zero mode does not require an
occupation choice for Z_p itself. This proves the
weak-family cap in the already given coupled-normal-energy convention without
using the old scalar C_p shortcut.

This does not yet identify that product with the original relative partition
functional. If the original denominator subtracts the free reference energy
instead of the coupled ground energy, the result contains the additional
factor exp[-ell(E_h-E_ref)] with its prescribed regularization. Shale
equivalence does not choose or cancel that factor. The original reference and
domain caller therefore remain necessary for a Z_rel identity.

The fixed-domain theorem in the original primitive source does provide a real
mathematical distinction: a bounded relative perturbation is realized on the
same modified-APS domain. In that family P0 remains the declared old boundary
datum and (5) is nontrivial. A moving choice P0=Ph makes the cap vacuum
trivial. This choice determines which polarizations are compared. It still
does not by itself prove that their Fock vector is one original QFT8 observable.

5. Constructive conclusion
For the actual weak h_p(t), no new source state needs to be selected. Use the
already fixed P0 and evaluate (5) on intervals of t between the finitely many
zero crossings. In a big-cell interval one may use the normalized Thouless
graph for efficient Wick calculations. At an overlap zero, switch charts by
extracting the finite particle-hole factor. At an h zero mode, retain the
original modified-APS zero-mode choice only when a definite coupled vacuum ray
or Thouless chart is needed; the cap itself is already unambiguous. This covers
the full common-Fock cap without declaring a new Gaussian source.

What remains open is narrower and original: exhibit the positive-time
admissible boundary insertion or limiting sequence whose OS class is this
already existing cap vector, and verify its reflection/support/domain labels.
Neither the Shale criterion nor the fixed-domain theorem alone supplies that
identification.
